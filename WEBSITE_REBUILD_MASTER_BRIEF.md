# Website Rebuild Master Brief

## Purpose

This document is the canonical redesign brief for Kiddo's public-facing website.
It is meant to replace scattered instincts, half-formed redesign ideas, and
section-by-section polishing with one coherent public-site strategy.

The standard is not "better than before."

The standard is:

- singular
- emotionally exact
- strategically rigorous
- visually memorable
- trust-forward without sounding defensive
- simple enough to feel inevitable

This brief is written as if the public site matters at trillion-dollar-company
level, because it does. A company this unusual cannot afford a website that is
merely informative or tasteful. The site must create recognition, trust,
emotional pull, and conversion without diffusing itself into a hundred good but
competing truths.

---

## The Core Thesis

Kiddo's public-site thesis is:

**The best gift for a child is one that lasts.**

Everything else supports this.

Not:

- "A better UTMA account"
- "A nicer brokerage for families"
- "A gifting app with investing"
- "A memory app with money"
- "A 529 alternative"

Those are supporting interpretations. They are not the public thesis.

The public identity should be:

**A child's investment fund built by the people who love them.**

That framing gives the company:

- `Gifting` as the wedge
- `Investing` as the engine
- `Memory Book` as the moat
- `Trust` as the permission structure
- `Age-18 handoff` as the payoff

If the site tries to lead with all five simultaneously, it weakens itself.
It should lead with one, then prove the rest.

---

## What Kiddo Is Really Selling

Kiddo is not primarily selling:

- asset allocation
- brokerage infrastructure
- tax optimization
- family finance literacy
- account setup

Kiddo is selling:

- a better family ritual
- a better first ownership experience
- a better way for generosity to compound
- a better emotional shape around a child account
- a better inheritance moment

This is crucial. The product mechanics matter, but the public site should speak
from the level of human value first, then descend into mechanics only when the
visitor asks for them.

---

## The Real Diagnosis

### What the site already has

- strong product truth
- unusually strong trust instincts
- emotional differentiation that is actually real
- much more substance than a typical startup site
- serious page coverage across pricing, FAQ, security, tools, compare, and gift flow

### What the site does not yet have

- one singular public thesis
- ruthless editorial compression
- iconic site-level visual identity
- enough atmosphere
- enough visual hierarchy
- enough confidence to let fewer ideas carry more weight
- enough separation between page roles

That is the real gap.

The site is not weak because the product is weak.
The site is weak because too many true things are competing for first position.

---

## Site Role Architecture

Every public page should have one main job.

### Home

Sell the thesis.

### Gift page

Convert the gifter with as little hesitation as possible.

### Pricing

Make paying feel fair, calm, and aligned.

### How It Works

Remove confusion quickly.

### Security

Close trust decisively and authoritatively.

### FAQ

Absorb objection depth and edge cases.

### Compare / tools / blog

Capture search traffic and evaluators.

### About

Deepen mission and emotional meaning without becoming a mechanics page.

Current problem: too many of these pages behave as if they are all partial
homepages.

Best version: each page does one job and hands off cleanly to the next.

---

## Primary Audience Hierarchy

The site is not equally for everyone.

Priority order:

1. Parent of a young child
2. Grandparent / aunt / uncle / family friend who may gift
3. Parent comparing alternatives
4. Trust-sensitive evaluator
5. Search visitor entering through a guide/tool/compare page
6. Partner / professional

This ordering matters because the homepage should not try to speak to all six in
equal volume. The homepage is for parents first, with enough gifter proof and
trust proof to support conversion.

---

## Homepage: Final Architecture

The homepage should have only seven sections.

1. Hero
2. Three proof pillars
3. Memory Book moat
4. Gift flow
5. Trust block
6. Age-18 payoff
7. Final CTA

Everything else is either:

- removed
- compressed heavily
- moved to a supporting page

The homepage should not carry the whole intelligence of the company.
It should carry the sharpest front door.

## What Leaves the Homepage

The homepage should not carry:

- deep strategy/mix explanation
- most pricing nuance
- 529 / FAFSA detail
- deep compare logic
- long "how it works" mechanics
- multiple layers of trust repetition
- broad educational copy
- too many feature cards

All of that can still exist. It just should not live on the front door.

The homepage exists to create:

- recognition
- emotional pull
- trust
- action

Not complete understanding.

---

## Visual Direction

The current site often feels like a highly thoughtful, polished startup site.

The new site should feel like:

- calm
- premium
- inevitable
- emotionally precise
- serious
- singular

This means:

- fewer cards
- fewer equal-weight modules
- more whitespace
- stronger typography hierarchy
- fewer screenshots, but much stronger ones
- less "page of sections" feeling
- more atmosphere and image authorship

The site should not become decorative.
It should become more distilled.

---

## The Four Flagship Assets

The public site should rely on four decisive visual assets.

### 1. Hero asset

One composed scene that communicates:

- a child
- family contribution
- real value accumulation
- trust
- product elegance
- emotional depth

This should not feel like a screenshot. It should feel like the company in one frame.

### 2. Gift asset

The cleanest, easiest, least intimidating giving moment possible.

This is the proof that the wedge is real.

### 3. Memory Book asset

The emotional moat in one unforgettable visual.

It should feel intimate, archival, and impossible for a plain brokerage to copy.

### 4. Age-18 asset

The handoff as inheritance.

This should not read as "settings + legal transition."
It should read as "this fund is becoming theirs."

---

## Screenshot and Image Strategy

Every image must create one specific belief.

Ask of each image:

- Why is it here?
- What belief is it supposed to create?
- Could this image replace three paragraphs of copy?

If not, it is likely filler.

Use image types intentionally:

- `Still image` for authority and iconicity
- `Tight crop` for proof of detail
- `Loop/GIF` for one interaction truth
- `Video/demo` only when live motion matters more than stillness

Do not default to device frames everywhere. Device frames should be a choice,
not a house style.

---

## Motion, Scroll, and Sensory Direction

The site should feel more expensive by doing less.

### Motion tiers

**Tier 1: Signature**

Rare. Reserved for hero or one emotional reveal.

**Tier 2: Functional**

Cards opening, CTA tactility, accordion motion, demo transitions.

**Tier 3: Ambient**

Nearly invisible. Felt more than noticed.

### Motion rules

- Hero gets one elegant entrance
- Emotional sections may breathe slightly
- Trust sections should barely move
- CTA motion should feel tactile, not flashy
- Scroll reveals should not become the personality of the site

### Scroll narrative

The homepage scroll should feel like:

1. thesis
2. proof
3. emotional deepening
4. trust stabilization
5. action

Not:

section -> section -> section -> section

---

## Video / GIF / Demo Rules

Moving media should only appear where motion proves something better than stills.

Good candidates:

- a gift arrives and the total updates
- a Memory Book entry opens
- a child sees "you own a piece of Disney"
- an age-18 reveal moment

Rules:

- each loop proves one belief only
- demos should be short
- motion should reduce explanation, not add another layer of it

Avoid:

- long product tours
- too many embedded demos
- generic startup motion wallpaper
- product motion for its own sake

---

## Copy System

The copy should be:

- warm
- adult
- concrete
- restrained
- quietly confident

Avoid:

- founder-memo energy
- over-poetry
- defensive trust language on primary pages
- repeating the same truth in multiple variants
- too many subordinate clauses

Default section pattern:

1. one strong headline
2. one clarifying paragraph
3. one proof object
4. one CTA

That is enough most of the time.

---

## Pricing Direction

Pricing should feel like a calm trust artifact.

Not:

- a defended argument
- a philosophy memo
- a detailed fee deposition

Keep:

- no platform fee on gifts
- AUM honesty
- refusal commitments
- clear plan differences

Reduce:

- repeated fee explanation
- too much qualification
- too much prose around every pricing choice

Best structure:

1. short headline
2. plans
3. one annual-fee explainer
4. "what we refuse to charge for"
5. compact FAQ
6. CTA

---

## How It Works Direction

How It Works should be a map, not a manual.

Best structure:

- For parents
- For gifters
- What happens to the money
- What the child gets later
- CTA

This page should answer:

"How does this work?"

Not:

"How does every part of the full operating system work?"

---

## Security Direction

Security should feel:

- sparse
- serious
- definitive
- authoritative

Structure:

1. What Kiddo is
2. What the broker-custodian layer does
3. What SIPC does and does not do
4. What happens if Kiddo disappears
5. Privacy posture
6. Contact / FAQ / BrokerCheck links

Less reassurance tone.
More calm authority.

---

## FAQ Direction

FAQ is where the site should absorb depth:

- objections
- rare cases
- edge-case comparison
- tax and FAFSA nuance
- custody anxiety

This is good. Keep the depth here.

But that depth should not leak back onto the homepage.

---

## Gift Page Direction

This is one of the most important public surfaces in the company.

Its ideal feeling:

**A grandparent can do this without hesitation.**

Priority outcomes:

- reduce option overload
- keep trust visible but not heavy
- make "your full gift reaches the child" unmistakable
- keep note/media emotional, not burdensome
- make the whole flow feel calmer and easier

The gift page should feel like the best expression of the wedge.

---

## CTA System

The whole public site should standardize around a small CTA family.

Primary:

- Start a fund

Secondary:

- See a real gift page
- See how it works
- Read how security works
- Read the FAQ

Avoid proliferating CTA types across pages. Repetition here creates confidence.

---

## Implementation Sequence

The recommended execution order is:

1. Lock the one-line thesis
2. Rebuild homepage around it
3. Cut homepage copy by 30-50%
4. Create the four flagship assets
5. Simplify Pricing
6. Shorten How It Works
7. Tighten Security
8. Refine Gift page trust/conversion balance
9. Reduce motion vocabulary
10. Unify CTA and visual logic site-wide

This order matters because visual and content refinement should follow the thesis,
not precede it.

---

## Success Criteria

The redesign should succeed if the site feels:

- more singular
- more legible
- more emotionally forceful
- more trustworthy
- more memorable
- less diffuse
- less section-stacked
- less eager to explain
- more inevitable

If the result is only "cleaner" or "prettier," it failed.

If the result feels like:

**a category-defining company**

instead of

**a brilliant startup explaining itself**

then it worked.

---

## Final Standard

The site does not need more ideas.

It needs:

- sharper selection
- stronger hierarchy
- deeper compression
- more confidence
- more atmosphere
- more visual authorship

That is the path from a very good public site to a truly elite one.
