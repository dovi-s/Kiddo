# Pricing / Revenue Model Options

**Status:** DRAFT for founder decision. This is analysis, not a decision, and
nothing here changes product or copy. It captures the strategy conversation so
it is durable and actionable.

**Date:** 2026-06-19

---

## TL;DR

The question is not "free or paid." The question is *when* you charge, and on
*what*. The answer that fits Kiddo's whole thesis: free for the family, forever,
and you make money on the money's movement and on the adult the child becomes,
never on the child's balance.

**Decision rule (the fork):**
- Loop compounds AND you will raise: go free.
- Loop compounds but you will not raise: charge the parent a small fee to
  survive to scale.
- Loop does not compound: pricing is irrelevant, fix the loop or fold.

Everything below supports that rule.

---

## 1. The reframe

Free is not lost revenue. For a business whose asset is a lifetime relationship
acquired at birth, free is the cheapest customer acquisition in fintech.

- Every other money app pays $100 to $400 to acquire a customer. Kiddo's gifting
  loop already acquires whole families at roughly zero.
- A subscription charged to a parent at setup taxes the one moment you cannot
  afford to tax: the moment of acquisition, which is also funded-k, the metric
  you most need to prove.
- The value of this business lives at the bottom of the funnel (the at-18
  handoff and the adult relationship after it), so charging at the top
  suppresses the thing that creates the value.

So the principle is: monetize the lifetime, and do it on the adult, never on the
child's balance.

---

## 2. Monetization engines, ranked by trust-alignment

Sort every idea by one test: does the user feel it, and does it touch the
child's money? The good engines are invisible to the user and never nibble the
balance.

| Engine | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Card / interchange | CORE (real engine), LATER to build | Merchant pays ~1.5% via a partner bank, invisible to the user. Proven by Chime, Greenlight, Cash App. Fires on teen and adult spend, so it depends on keeping the kid. The teen card becomes the graduate's first adult card. |
| Float / net interest on cash | LATER (arrives with the wallet) | Settlement, dividends, spend balances earn interest at scale. Natural float only, never engineered idle cash. |
| The adult OS after 18 | CORE (the windfall) | The kept kid becomes an adult who needs a Roth, brokerage, first card, eventually a mortgage. Monetize the way a bank does. This is where LTV explodes against ~$0 CAC. |
| Institutions fund the seed | CHASE | Employers (family benefit), states (baby bonds), federal Trump Accounts. Someone other than the family funds the account; Kiddo is the experience layer. Free distribution, on-thesis, timely. |
| Premium keepsakes / ceremony | CHASE NOW (first real dollars, no license) | Monetize meaning, not money: physical card, framed milestone, printed Memory Book. High margin, deepens the moat, grandparents buy it. |
| Fiduciary referral at the handoff | CHASE | At 18 the graduate needs first adult products; an 18-year trust relationship earns the right to recommend, fiduciary, not paid placement. Stays advice, never a kickback machine. |
| Goodwill sponsor matches | CHASE LATER | A brand funds a match (marketing / CSR budget); Kiddo runs the campaign. Never lets the sponsor influence what the kid invests in (that is advice and liability). |
| AUM fee (0.10%) | LATER, probably waive | Most-aligned fee but slow and small (needs mass users and large balances), and it muddies the cleanest trust line, "never a fee on your child's money." |
| Optional parent power-user tier | OPTION (intent filter) | A small fee for advanced family controls, never touching the child's money. Honest, predictable, filters for intent. This is the fallback if you do not raise. |

### Kill list (trust is the only moat)
- Withdrawal fees and early-withdrawal fees: charge the child's money at the
  keystone moment, self-sabotage on your own prize.
- Mandated idle cash for rebalancing: a hidden tax, cash drag on the kid.
- Selling data: kills the only moat outright.
- PFOF: controversial, wrong for a trust brand.
- Kiddo-funded match: buys deposits the loop already earns for free.
- Generic "rails for everyone" (powering any fundraiser): commoditizes you.
  Open rails only where they acquire a family into your graph.

## 3. The unlock: a Kiddo balance

One primitive makes free gifting, float, and the card all true at once: a Kiddo
balance / wallet.

- This is the Cash App insight. In-network transfers are free because they are
  ledger entries, not card rails. If gift money lands in a Kiddo balance,
  gifter-to-fund becomes a free ledger move (real-free gifting, no processing to
  absorb).
- The balance throws off float.
- A card spends it (interchange), and the card is the bridge that carries the
  kid into the adult relationship.

It is also exactly the "regulated core" the company strategy already says needs
strategic capital (money-transmitter or sponsor-bank buildout). So the wallet is
the long-term engine, not a near-term move.

## 4. The three cruxes that actually gate the decision

The free-versus-paid debate is downstream of three empirical questions. Resolve
these before betting the model.

### Crux 1: Does the kid keep the account at 18? (retention)
The whole big-outcome thesis rests on an 18-year-old keeping the money app a
parent picked. Default outcome is churn or dormancy, because the relationship
was with the parent, not the kid.
- **Cheap test:** instrument teen engagement (do 13-17 year olds open the kid
  view unprompted) as the leading indicator, and recruit a small near-18 cohort
  to watch the actual handoff. Study Step's aging-out cohort for outside signal.
- **Implication:** if teens do not engage, do not bet on the handoff, monetize
  the parent. The fix, if there is one, is to make the kid the customer years
  before 18 (their own login, education, card, friends). That work is currently
  unfunded.

### Crux 2: Will families fund kid investing with real, recurring money,
unprompted, at scale? (demand)
One-time emotional gift is easy. Compounding (a kid funded repeatedly by
multiple people over years, without nagging) is the real claim and is unproven.
- **Cheap test, fully available now, zero new build:** of funded kids, what
  share get a second gift from a third person without a reminder; median dollars
  per kid per year; does it survive turning reminders off; funded-rate by
  channel (birth/registry vs faith-community/bar-mitzvah).
- **Implication:** this is the gate. If it does not compound, nothing else
  matters. You likely already half-know this number; go look at it first.

### Crux 3: Can you reach the scale where free pays before you run out of
runway? (viability)
Interchange and float only pay at scale and need the regulated buildout.
Interchange on kids is near zero (kids barely spend), so it depends on Crux 1.
- **Cheap test:** it is arithmetic plus a fundraising temperature check. Model
  burn, runway, and the scale-where-free-pays curve. It will almost certainly
  show free requires raising.
- **Implication:** free as a business model is a post-raise strategy.

**The cruxes are coupled:** Crux 3 depends on Crux 1, which depends on building
the teen relationship, which costs money (Crux 3). Crux 2 sits under all of it.

---

## 5. The contradiction to resolve

"Go fully free" and "prove the loop and do not raise" cannot both be true.
Free's revenue (card, float, adult OS) only exists after a raise and after
years of scale. So either:
- You raise, and free is fine, or
- You do not raise, and you need the parent to pay now, and "free forever" waits
  until after a raise.

There is no version where free, no raise, and survival all hold.

---

## 6. Sequencing (recommended)

The pricing decision should be the last thing decided, not the first.

1. **This week, free, zero build:** pull the Crux 2 loop numbers. This needs no
   pricing decision and it gates everything.
2. **This week, on a spreadsheet:** model Crux 3 (burn, runway, scale-where-
   free-pays). This tells you whether free is affordable without a raise.
3. **Start now, because it is the long pole:** instrument teen engagement and
   manufacture a near-18 cohort (Crux 1). You cannot shortcut 18 years, so start
   the clock.
4. **Then decide pricing** using the fork in the TL;DR.

## 7. Near-term launch reality (the "just ship it" path)

The instinct to wire Stripe + domain and soft-launch to get the real signal is
right. Two clarifications:

- **Stripe live, domain, email** are config/ops and genuinely fast.
- **Live custodial investing via Alpaca production is NOT a tomorrow flip.** It
  is a partnership onboarding gated on securities counsel (see
  `COUNSEL_ENGAGEMENT_PACKET.md`). Sandbox is instant but is not real money.
- **You do not need live investing to get the signal that matters.** The loop
  (Crux 2) is about whether people gift, not whether it is invested yet.
  Soft-launch the gifting loop with investing framed as going live shortly,
  capture intent (the capture-at-intent path is built; have counsel bless the
  exact mechanism), and measure repeat funding. Keep the existing subscription
  for now; pricing is reversible and does not need to block launch.
- **The one line not to cross cheaply:** taking real money to "invest" a minor's
  funds before custody and counsel are real. Stay on the safe side (capture
  intent, clear disclosure, counsel sign-off) and the rest is genuinely ship-
  and-see.

---

## 8. The bet, in one line

Free is how you win the graph, and the graph is the company. Everything
monetizable sits downstream of owning it. The only near-term question that
matters is whether free reaches funded-k before runway runs out. If yes, there
is no pricing problem, just the best CAC in fintech on a graph nobody can buy.
