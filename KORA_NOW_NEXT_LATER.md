# Kora Now / Next / Later

Updated: 2026-04-12

Purpose:
- give Kora one operating plan instead of scattered ideas
- define what matters now, what unlocks next, and what is later
- keep the team focused on the highest-leverage sequence

Use this when the question is:
- what are we actually prioritizing right now?
- what should not distract us yet?
- what has to be true before the next layer unlocks?

## Core View

Kora is strongest when it is treated as:
- a child-first investment gifting product
- powered by a gifter-to-parent growth loop
- strengthened by trust, retention, and simple recurring usage

Everything else should serve that.

## Now

### 1. Make the core product unquestionably work

Focus:
- parent onboarding
- gifter checkout
- first gift success
- post-gift experience
- dashboard clarity after setup

Why now:
- if the core product leaks, every future distribution channel amplifies the leak
- this is the foundation for trust, retention, and referrals

Success looks like:
- parents can create and share a fund with confidence
- gifters can complete a gift quickly on mobile
- the first gift creates a live, emotionally meaningful product state

### 2. Tighten the gifter-to-parent loop

Focus:
- post-gift CTA
- attribution from gifter to parent signup
- deep linking and share-link reliability
- gifter confirmation and follow-up

Why now:
- this is the cheapest and most native acquisition channel Kora has
- it proves the product can help distribute itself

Success looks like:
- some gifters become new hosts
- loop-generated parents behave at least as well as normal parents

### 3. Build the retention floor

Focus:
- recurring parent contributions
- Memory Book prompts and milestone moments
- dashboard nudges that lead to action

Why now:
- gifting occasions are episodic
- Kora needs a behavior between occasions so funds do not feel dormant

Success looks like:
- parents return between gifting events
- funds keep moving even when family is not actively gifting

### 4. Keep trust and compliance legible

Focus:
- clean trust copy
- clear explanation of Kora vs DriveWealth roles
- clear handoff and ownership framing

Why now:
- this is a financial product for children
- trust is not a supporting element, it is the product

Success looks like:
- parents and gifters do not hesitate because the structure feels unclear

## Next

### 5. Add credibility-led distribution

Focus:
- DriveWealth intros
- adviser intros
- one partnership-ready one-pager
- one clear demo script

Why next:
- credibility should come after the product converts on its own
- this creates warmer openings into community and institutional channels

Success looks like:
- intros lead to meetings, follow-ups, and one or two real champion paths

### 6. Run 1 to 3 school pilots

Focus:
- one narrow pilot format
- school-specific landing flow
- school cohort reporting
- school-safe share language

Why next:
- schools create trust and dense distribution
- they are a better early partnership tier than employers or brands

Success looks like:
- one school champion can drive parent signups and first gifts in a measurable way

### 7. Prepare employer packaging, not broad employer sales

Focus:
- employer narrative
- employer landing and cohort attribution
- employer scorecard

Why next:
- employers are powerful but need a cleaner package than early consumer growth
- this work should start after schools show trust-based distribution can work

Success looks like:
- Kora is ready for one employer design partner, not a large employer motion

## Later

### 8. Scale employer benefits

Focus:
- one employer design partner
- measurable cohort performance
- repeatable onboarding and support

Why later:
- employer benefits should scale what is already working
- they are for bulk acquisition, not early discovery

Success looks like:
- employer-origin cohorts activate efficiently and beat paid acquisition economics

### 9. Run brand partnerships only when conversion is ready

Focus:
- campaign landing variants
- channel quality reporting
- strict conversion guardrails

Why later:
- brands create reach, but reach without conversion is vanity
- brand energy is only useful once the system underneath is strong

Success looks like:
- partnership traffic becomes activated funds, not just visits

### 10. Broader platform expansion

Focus:
- personal-fund expansion only if child-fund wedge is strong
- wider "send stock instead of cash" vision only after the child-fund system is clearly winning

Why later:
- Kora wins first by being focused
- expansion is upside, not the immediate operating plan

Success looks like:
- new surfaces extend the core engine instead of diluting it

## What We Are Not Doing Right Now

Do not let these become distractions:
- broad enterprise sales
- large school district deals before pilots work
- major employer sales motion before packaging is ready
- splashy brand campaigns before activation is strong
- over-expanding investment complexity
- turning Kora into a generic fintech app

## Working Sequence

The actual sequence is:

1. Core product
2. Gifter-to-parent loop
3. Retention floor
4. Trust and credibility layer
5. Adviser / DriveWealth intros
6. School pilots
7. Employer prep
8. Employer scale
9. Brand reach

That is the operating sequence.

## Decision Filter

For any new idea, ask:

1. Does this improve core conversion?
2. Does this strengthen the gifter loop?
3. Does this increase retention between occasions?
4. Does this increase trust enough to unlock the next channel?
5. If not, is it truly important right now?

If the answer is no, it is probably later.

## One-Sentence Company Plan

Kora should win by making child investment gifting work beautifully on its own, then turning that into a trusted distribution flywheel before scaling into larger channels.
