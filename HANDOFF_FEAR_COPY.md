# Handoff-Fear Copy — a proposal (founder owns the voice)

*2026-06-10. The keystone reframe (`COMPANY_STRATEGY.md` §0b, `VOICE_OF_CUSTOMER.md`)
made concrete in parent-facing words: **confront** the handoff fear instead of
assuming the handoff reads as good. PROPOSAL ONLY — brand voice is founder-owned;
tune or reject. Written em-dash-free and on-voice so it can drop straight into
product. The honesty discipline below is load-bearing: it never promises a kid
won't cash out (the money is legally theirs at majority, and a false promise would
burn the trust that IS the moat). It earns the keep-it choice instead.*

---

## The core line (hero / section headline options)

- **"Most accounts hand an 18-year-old a check. We spend eighteen years making
  sure they're ready for it."**
- "The hardest part of giving a child money isn't the money. It's the day it
  becomes theirs."
- "Every custodial account becomes your child's at 18. The only question that
  matters is whether they're ready."

## "What happens when it becomes theirs?" (the section that confronts the fear)

> Every custodial account does the same thing on paper: at 18 or 21, the money is
> legally your child's. The question every parent actually asks is quieter. Will
> they be ready?
>
> Most accounts can't answer that, because the day before the handoff, your child
> was never really part of it. A balance simply appears in an app they have never
> opened.
>
> Kiddo is built backwards from that day. For years before it, your child watches
> their fund grow, sees who showed up for them, learns how investing works from
> their own money, and reads the notes the people who love them left along the
> way. So when it becomes theirs, it is not a windfall handed to a stranger. It is
> something they have understood, and been loved through, for as long as they can
> remember.
>
> We cannot make the choice for them. It is their money, and that is the point.
> But a child who has owned something for eighteen years, and knows exactly who
> built it and why, tends to keep building it. That is the whole reason we exist.

## FAQ: "What if they just blow it all at 18?"

> It is the fear every parent has, and it is the reason we built Kiddo the way we
> did.
>
> By law, a custodial account becomes your child's at the age of majority. No app
> can change that, and we would not want one that could. What we can do is spend
> the years before it making the account something they understand and care about,
> instead of a surprise check.
>
> A child who watched it grow, chose some of what is inside, and read a note from
> their grandfather about it is a very different eighteen-year-old than one handed
> a number. We cannot promise what they will do. We can promise we spent eighteen
> years preparing them to do it well.

## Short version (for the "What happens when they turn 18?" tile / handoff surfaces)

> Every custodial account becomes your child's at 18 or 21. The difference is
> whether they are ready for it. Kiddo spends the years before making it something
> they understand and were loved through, so it lands as a head start, not a
> windfall.

## The honesty discipline (why this lands)

Never promise a child won't cash out. It is legally theirs at majority, and a
false guarantee would burn the exact trust that is the moat. Every line above
earns the keep-it choice through eighteen years of education + relationship +
ownership, and is honest that the choice is ultimately the child's. That honesty
is what separates this from a sales pitch, and it is why a wary parent believes
it. The fear is real; the answer is not "trust us, they won't," it is "here is
everything we did so they would not want to."
