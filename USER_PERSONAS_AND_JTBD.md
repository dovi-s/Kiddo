# Who uses Kiddo, what they want, and what would help us win

> A POV (not data) on every user type: what they want to see/know, the mental
> models they bring, what they'd love and fear about Kiddo, what they love/hate
> about the alternatives, and what would help the company succeed. Grounded in
> the product + the locked strategy (gifter loop, Memory Book moat, kid-at-18
> lens, substitutes-are-the-real-rival). Written 2026-05-29 as a thinking
> artifact — argue with it, don't treat it as gospel.

---

## 1. The Parent (account holder / custodian — the buyer)

**Their real job:** "Be the parent who shows up — give my kid a real head start —
without it becoming another chore I feel guilty about." Underneath: *am I a good
provider?* and *is this safe with my kid's money and SSN?*

**Want to see / know on arrival:** Is this legit (who holds the money, am I liable,
is it insured)? How much does it cost and is there a catch? How is this different
from just opening a free Fidelity UTMA? What happens at 18? Can I undo this?

**Familiar with (mental models they bring):** Venmo (send money), a 529 (college),
a savings account, "stocks are risky/complicated," Acorns ("round-ups"), the
group-chat birthday-money-collection. They are NOT familiar with UTMA mechanics or
the kiddie tax — and they don't want to become experts.

**Loves about Kiddo:** the gifter link ("I don't have to be the only one funding
this"), the Memory Book (the part that makes them tear up — "Emma reads this at
18"), Kid View (their kid actually engaging), the honesty (no fake numbers, "not
guaranteed" everywhere).

**Hates / fears:** "Is my money actually invested or is this a startup that'll
vanish?" (the EarlyBird-got-acquired fear), the SSN ask (huge trust moment),
complexity creep ("wait, what's free vs Plus vs Family again?"), and any whiff of
"another subscription."

**Loves/hates about alternatives:** *Fidelity/Schwab UTMA* — trusts the brand, hates
that it's cold and gifting is a paper-check ordeal. *529* — loves the tax break,
hates the college-only restriction. *Acorns Early* — likes the ecosystem, feels
round-ups are "spare change, not a head start." *EarlyBird* — loved it, got burned
when it was absorbed.

**What helps us win them:** (1) **Trust, made unmissable** — custody live +
"Member FINRA/SIPC, held by our broker-dealer partner, not Kiddo," the at-18
plan visible up front. (2) The honest "why not a free UTMA/529?" answer rehearsed
everywhere: *the loop + the Memory Book + the ritual + it's theirs at 18 for
anything*. (3) Protect simplicity — the moment the parent feels confused about
tiers, you've lost the "soda-a-month" ease.

## 2. The Gifter (grandparent / aunt / friend — the GROWTH ENGINE)

**Their real job:** "Give something that actually matters — not another toy that's
junk by Tuesday — and show this kid I love them, in under a minute, without
signing up for anything."

**Want to see / know:** Whose kid is this (a face/name, reassurance I'm in the
right place)? How much should I give? Does it really go to the kid? Is my card
safe? Can I add a note/voice (the emotional payload)?

**Familiar with:** writing a check in a card, Venmo, "I don't do apps," Amazon
registries. The median gifter is **older and tech-wary** — every extra tap or
required field is a real drop-off.

**Loves about Kiddo:** *no account needed* (the whole thing), Apple Pay (familiar +
fast), attaching a voice memo (the part that makes it feel like more than money),
seeing "$50 could be $X at 18" (impact). It makes them feel *generous and smart*.

**Hates / fears:** "Do I have to make an account?" (deal-breaker if yes — you got
this right), "am I picking stocks now?? I just want to give $50" (you default this
away — right), card-security doubt, and confusion about whether it worked.

**Loves/hates about alternatives:** *A check* — familiar, but they KNOW it gets
spent/forgotten. *Stockpile gift card* — clunky redemption. *Cash in a card* —
disappears. Gifters secretly *want* a better option; they just won't tolerate
friction to use it.

**What helps us win them:** This is the **k≥1 engine** — protect it ferociously.
Zero forced fields, one-tap pay (enable Google/Apple too), the note/voice front and
center, an unmistakable "it worked + here's the impact" confirmation, and the
*reminder* that brings them back next birthday. The gifter is the cheapest customer
acquisition you will ever have — never charge them, never make them think.

## 3. The Kid (5–17, viewing — the relationship / future owner)

**Their real job (age-banded):** 5–8: "is this *mine*? what do I own?" (wonder).
9–13: "what's a stock, did my money grow, that's cool." 14–17: "can I have a say?"

**Want to see / know:** What do I own (companies I recognize — Disney, Roblox), is
it really mine, how much, who gave it to me (the Memory Book), what happens when
I'm older.

**Familiar with:** Roblox/games, brands they love, YouTube — NOT finance. Wonder and
ownership are the hooks, not returns.

**Loves about Kiddo:** "I own a piece of Disney," seeing gifts from people who love
them, the at-18 countdown, the sealed letter. This is where **the relationship**
(your real moat) is built — a kid who grew up watching *their* fund is not going to
ACAT it to Schwab at 18.

**Hates / fears:** being talked down to, or being made to feel dumb about money. The
copy that respects them (age-aware, "you own a tiny piece of the place where those
games get played") is exactly right.

**What helps us win them:** Kid View free on every plan (locked — right; the kid
didn't choose the tier). Accuracy (we just fixed the Roblox-ownership and kiddie-tax
copy — *because this is the one place the user is a child, getting it wrong is
worse*). The kid is a 13-year compounding relationship, not a quarterly metric.

## 4. The Kid AT 18 (the handoff — the kid-2.0 flywheel)

**Their real job:** "Wait, this is real and it's *mine*? What do I do — and don't
make me feel stupid." Emotional climax + a genuine fork: cash it out (the failure
mode — same as a cash gift would've been) or keep going (the win).

**Want/know:** what's mine, what are my options, what's the tax hit if I sell, what
happens if I do nothing (answer: it keeps compounding — good).

**What helps us win them:** the orientation walkthrough (Age18Welcome) landing
*calmly and accurately* — this is why the kiddie-tax fix mattered. Nail this and
they roll into Roth/adult products (kid-2.0). Botch it and they liquidate for a car
and you've produced a cash gift with extra steps. **This single moment is the whole
lifetime-value thesis.**

## 5. The Co-parent / guardian (invited collaborator)

**Job:** "I'm the other parent — let me see and help without re-creating everything."
Wants: shared visibility, ability to contribute/manage, not a second account silo.
**Win:** co-parent access in Plus (right), clean invite, no double-charge. Hate to
avoid: feeling like a second-class viewer.

## 6. The Skeptic (sophisticated parent / advisor-minded)

**Job:** "Convince me this beats a free Fidelity UTMA + a 529." Brings real finance
knowledge; allergic to fluff and fake projections. **Loves:** the honesty (fee-
netted projections, "not guaranteed," "Kiddo is not a broker-dealer"). **Hates:**
any overclaim (which is why the whole copy sweep mattered to this persona most).
**Win:** the Porter answer — we don't beat Fidelity on custody/cost; we win on the
*loop + Memory Book + ritual + at-18*. Concede returns, win on job. Never bluff a
sophisticated parent — they're also your loudest referrer or harshest reviewer.

## 7. The CPA / financial advisor (tax season, on behalf of the family)

**Job:** "Reconcile this for a return without a headache." Lands on FundSnapshot /
TaxDocuments. **Wants:** cost basis, realized gains, the 1099 story, kiddie-tax
clarity, CSV export. **Loves:** CSV + honest "broker's 1099 is authoritative."
**Hates:** vague or wrong tax claims (kiddie tax, average-cost-vs-FIFO — both fixed).
**Win:** be the UTMA platform a CPA *doesn't dread*. An advisor who trusts the data
becomes a quiet distribution channel.

## 8. The institutional / at-scale gifter (schools, employers, registries, congregations)

**Job:** "Give to many kids at once / let our community fund a kid" — a bar mitzvah
with 40 gifters, a hospital's baby program, an employer benefit, a Babylist registry
tile. **Want:** bulk/partner mechanics, attribution, no per-gift account friction.
**Today:** you have the traffic mechanics (links + UTM) but lack a `partnerSource`
attribution primitive (gated, per the Babylist plan). **Win:** occasions are already
viral-shaped (one event → dozens of gifters → dozens of new funds). Build the
partner-attribution primitive and this becomes a *second* growth loop on top of the
organic gifter loop.

## What would help us SUCCEED as a company (the cross-cutting truth)

1. **Make the gifter loop compound (k ≥ 1).** This is the entire business. EarlyBird
   died on $200+ parent CAC; the loop is the only path to near-zero CAC. Everything
   else is secondary. (You already measure it — the k-factor panel. Watch it like
   the one vital sign.)
2. **The moat is the relationship + the un-ACAT-able Memory Book**, not features.
   Every gift's voice note and the kid's 13-year attachment are switching costs
   Fidelity can't replicate. Invest there; don't compete on cost (Target, not Walmart).
3. **Trust is the launch gate.** Custody live + legal (RIA/COPPA) memo + honest
   custody copy (done) is what converts the wary parent and unlocks the SSN ask.
4. **Protect simplicity — the silent killer is complexity creep.** The product
   already does a lot; every new surface taxes the parent's "soda-a-month" ease. The
   frozen polish backlog is the right instinct.
5. **Reduce friction where the loop lives:** one-tap social login (gifter→parent),
   zero forced fields on the gift, the reminder that brings gifters back.
6. **Nail the at-18 handoff** — it's both the emotional proof of the whole premise
   and the entry to the kid-2.0 lifetime funnel (Roth, banking).
7. **Never bluff.** The honesty discipline this whole sweep enforced isn't just
   compliance — it's the brand. Sophisticated parents, CPAs, and journalists all
   punish a fintech that overclaims, and reward one that doesn't.

The one-sentence version: **win by being the platform that turns the people who
already love a kid into a near-zero-CAC growth loop, then locks them in with a
relationship and a Memory Book no brokerage can copy — and never lie to any of them.**
