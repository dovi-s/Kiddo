# Kiddo — Company Strategy Spine

*The one-page answer to "what business are we in and what do we do, in what
order." Written to be steered by and handed to a cofounder or investor.*

**Date:** 2026-06-02 · **Status:** founder-level strategic frame, not a task list.

---

## 0. The one sentence

**Kiddo is the cheapest acquisition funnel ever built for the next generation's
primary financial relationship.** Gifting is the wedge. The 18-year-old at
handoff is the prize. The 0.10% AUM fee is the meter that proves the
relationship exists — it is *not* the business.

---

## 1. What business we are NOT in (and why the obvious framing is a trap)

"A kids' investing app" caps us in a small, crowded niche and makes the math
look weak: at 0.10%, AUM *alone* needs ~$1T in assets to be a $1B revenue line.
If the story is "charge 10 bps on kids' UTMAs," this is not obviously a
venture-scale business, and we should know that cold. Every competitor that
took that framing (EarlyBird, UNest, Acorns Early) is dead, stalled, or a
feature. They all share one structural flaw: **they end at 18.**

*(Precision note, 2026-06-07 — say it exactly, because a sharp investor will
check: Acorns technically migrates the balance into an adult Acorns Invest
account at the age of transfer. They keep the ACCOUNT; they never had the
RELATIONSHIP. Their 18-year-old gets a cold app with a balance — the kid was
never the user, there's no memory, no history of being loved through it. The
flaw is relational, not mechanical, and the precise version is the defensible
one.)*

## 2. What business we ARE in (the reframe everything hangs on)

At the at-18 handoff we hold something nobody else in this space has:

- a young adult who **suddenly has money** and is becoming financially active
  for the first time,
- **18 years of accumulated trust**,
- a **Memory Book** of emotional switching cost that can't be ACAT'd or copied.

Robinhood, Chase, every bank on earth pays enormous CAC to acquire exactly that
person. **We get them for free, ~18 years early, with maximum trust, at the
precise moment they go financially active.** That is the asset. Gifting is just
the funnel that originates it.

This reframe does three things at once:

1. **Fixes the "AUM is too small" problem.** The fee proves the relationship;
   the *business* is the lifetime financial relationship (Roth, banking,
   brokerage, the parent-2.0 re-subscription). The fee is a rounding error next
   to that.
2. **Turns our biggest fear into our inflection.** The "revenue cliff at 18"
   isn't the death — it's the **handoff to the real business.** 18 is where it
   starts, not where it ends.
3. **Creates a real moat.** A loop a giant can copy in a quarter is not a moat.
   *18 years of trust + a Memory Book at the moment of financial activation* is
   genuinely uncopyable by a bolt-on feature. But this moat only exists if we
   **build the company around the handoff**, not around the gift.

## 2.5 The invariants under the bets

Each load-bearing bet rests on something that won't move in 10 years. Worth
naming, because it tells us where long-term effort is safe to sink:

- People give gifts to children they love, and resent that most of it is
  landfill. → the wedge.
- Every cohort turns 18 and goes financially active; incumbents will keep
  paying top-dollar CAC for that person. → the prize.
- Records of being loved appreciate with age and can't be transferred out
  with the assets. → the moat.
- Compounding needs time; childhood is the longest runway a person gets.
  → the meter.

What *isn't* stable: custody vendors, payment rails, regulatory detail,
channels, the fee structure itself. Those we rent or keep swappable — already
doctrine. Sink effort into the first list, keep optionality on the second.

## 3. The North Star + the one metric that gates everything

- **North Star:** own the lifetime financial relationship (kid who received →
  adult who owns → parent who gives), originated at near-zero CAC.
- **The gate:** **funded-k ≥ 1.** Does a funded gift generate a new funded
  family? Until that number exists and is ≥1, this is a *feature*, not a
  company — no amount of product, polish, or AUM math changes that. Everything
  we do should either move funded-k or be free.

## 4. The honest sequence (do NOT invert this)

**Phase 1 — Prove the engine (now, no outside capital).**
Prove the loop turns (funded-k) *and* find ONE repeatable, cheap distribution
channel. Bootstrap. **Do not raise yet** — raising on an unproven loop is
exactly how EarlyBird died: capital → growth pressure → paid parent CAC ($200+,
~25-mo payback) → bleed out. The product is already built; the bottleneck is
distribution + loop proof, not features.

**The pond rule (added 2026-06-06, founder-approved — the Thiel sharpening).**
Phase 1's growth shape is *total domination of one tiny market*, never a broad
launch. Every monopoly starts in a market so small nobody else considers it a
market (PayPal: eBay power-sellers; Facebook: one campus). Ours: **one
occasion × one dense community** where cash-gifting to kids is already
cultural and everyone attends everyone's events — a metro's bar/bat-mitzvah
circuit, a large church's baby-shower network, one big school community.
Pond-selection criteria: (a) cash gifts to kids are normative, (b) social
density (the same gifters recur across events), (c) a calendar of upcoming
occasions to ride. The win metric is NOT user count — it's **% of the
community's kid-money occasions that default to a Kiddo link** (target:
effectively all of them) plus funded-k *inside the pond*. A market that small
is invisible to incumbents (can't move their needle = our protection), and it
is the one place our honest network-effect candidate ignites: in a dense
community the gift graph is LOCAL — each family added makes Kiddo more useful
to the gifters who know them, and the multi-kid gifter dashboard becomes the
gifter's own ledger. Expansion is concentric and graph-led (gifters in pond A
have grandkids in pond B), never broadcast. 100% of something tiny beats 1%
of something huge. **Forbidden under this rule: any "national launch,"
broad-channel push, or paid acquisition before a first pond is owned.**

**Phase 2 — Buy the regulated core (after funded-k ≥ 1 + one working channel).**
Raise *strategic* capital — earmarked for the regulated foundation (custody,
the narrow legal opinion, compliance) and the at-18 / kid-2.0 expansion,
**not** for growth. If growth still needs to be bought at this point, the
thesis was wrong.

**Phase 3 — Become the financial OS for a generation.**
Banking, Roth, the parent-2.0 loop closing on itself. This is the venture-scale
story. Everything before it is earning the right to attempt it.

## 5. The strategic forks worth deciding deliberately

- **B2B2C vs. consumer brand.** Fighting for consumer attention is slow and
  expensive. The faster scale path may be embedding "gift-investing-as-a-feature"
  where gifting *already happens*: registries (Babylist), hospitals/pediatric,
  employer baby-benefits, banks/credit unions, advisors with wealthy
  grandparent clients. Seriously weigh whether Kiddo's best shape is
  *infrastructure others embed* rather than a brand we market. It changes who we
  hire and what we build.
- **Audience/media as a company pillar, not a tactic.** A dead competitor proves
  the *product* is hard; nobody can stop us from owning the *audience* of
  gift-minded grandparents and values-driven parents. That list becomes launch
  demand, an owned ~$0-CAC channel, and a durable asset. Most fintechs treat
  content as marketing exhaust; for us it can be core.
- **Sell the funnel, not the fee.** If the at-18 customer is the real asset, the
  pitch to investors and partners is "we originate the next generation of
  financial customers," not "we take 10 bps." Bigger, more fundable, more honest.

## 6. The big risks — named, not buried

- **The loop may have k < 1** → then it's a feature, not a company. Unresolved.
  The whole ballgame. Resolve it cheaply before anything else.
- **Incumbent copy risk** → if a giant with distribution adds "gift to a kid,"
  our only defense is the 18-year trust + handoff ownership. So we *must* build
  the company around the handoff, or we have no moat at all.
- **Capital/regulatory drag** → we're slower and more regulated than a
  feature-bolt-on competitor. The only counter is relationship depth they can't
  fake — again, real only if we orient around it.

## 7. What this means we should STOP doing

Treating Kiddo as a product-completeness problem. The product is done enough.
The company's bottleneck is **distribution + loop proof.** Per our own locked
strategy: polish is frozen, funded-k is the mission metric. The highest-leverage
move available today is to actually obey the rule we already wrote — reorganize
attention around reading k and finding one channel, and put everything that
doesn't move that number off the table until it exists.

### TL;DR for the busy reader
Kiddo is not a kids' gifting app with a cliff at 18. It's a zero-CAC machine for
acquiring a generation's primary financial relationship — gifting is the wedge,
the at-18 handoff is the product, AUM is the meter. Prove the loop (funded-k≥1),
find one cheap channel, *then* raise to build the regulated core and the
lifetime expansion. In that order. Don't raise early; don't polish; don't end
at 18.
