# Kiddo — Handoff-Retention Pilot Run-Sheet

*The cheapest test of gate #2: do the kids STAY at handoff? Collapses an 18-year
question into a 1–3-year one by recruiting near-majority families and running
real handoffs. Sibling to `LOOP_TEST_RUNSHEET.md` (gate #1, the loop).*

**Owner:** founder · **Time-box:** 6–12 months per cohort · **Created:** 2026-06-10
**Governs:** the Phase-2 "prove they stay" step. Strategy: `MOAT_LEADING_INDICATORS.md`
(gate #2). Measurement surface: `/admin` moat leading-indicators panel.

---

## 0. The one question

**At majority (18/21), does the kid keep the account and take the next product,
or cash out and leave?**

Gate #1 (the loop) gets you the kids. Gate #2 decides whether they're a moat or a
one-time feature. A kid who cashes out at 18 is the EarlyBird outcome: same as a
cash gift, and you're a worse Acorns. This is the load-bearing unproven bet of
the whole company, and the one you can least afford to discover at year 18.

## 1. The behaviors + the numbers

Per handed-off kid, at 6 and 12 months post-handoff:

| # | Behavior | The number |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Kept** the account (no full withdrawal / no ACAT out) | % kept |
| 2 | **Active** (logged in, looked, did anything) vs dormant | % active |
| 3 | **Added** their own money (even $1) | % contributed |
| 4 | **Took the next product** (Roth when income exists, banking) | % converted |
| 5 | **Cashed out** (full withdrawal within 6 mo) | % churned |

The headline is **% kept AND active at 6 months.** #4 (next-product take) is the
upside read; #5 (fast cash-out) is the alarm.

## 2. The mechanism (you do NOT need a kid born into Kiddo)

Retention-at-handoff does not care that they weren't born into it. Recruit
families whose kid turns 18/21 within ~12 months, onboard them, let real gifts +
the Memory Book accrue for the months you have, then run the **real handoff** (the
keepsake freeze, the `/welcome-at-18` walkthrough, the Roth nudge) and watch.

**Gating:** the *full* test needs live custody (a real account they could
actually keep or cash out, and a real Roth to take). Pre-custody you can run a
**lighter sentiment version** — simulate the handoff, measure stated intent + the
leading indicators — but treat it as directional only; stated intent over-reads.

## 3. Who to recruit (~10–15 near-majority families)

- Your own network first (warm, honest, forgiving of rough edges).
- Families already saving for a 16–17-year-old some other way (cash, a UTMA
  elsewhere, savings bonds) — they have the "people invested in me" history, just
  not via Kiddo. These are the best analog to a real cohort.
- Skip anyone who'd treat it as a favor to you. You need honest behavior, not
  politeness.

## 4. The pre-commit (the most important section — set this NOW)

**Write the kill line before you have a single beloved pilot family.** Once you
have 20 kids you've onboarded by hand, you will rationalize any retention number.
The only credible time to pre-commit is now, while you have zero skin in it.

**Kill line (pre-committed 2026-06-10):**
> If **> 1/3 of a handed-off cohort cashes out or goes dormant within 6 months**,
> the relationship-origination moat is in serious doubt — *regardless* of how good
> the loop, the Memory Book, or the demo looks. That triggers a hard rethink of
> whether this is a generational platform or a gifting feature, not a "tune the
> copy and re-run" reflex.

**Green line:** ≥ 2/3 kept AND active at 6 months, with any non-zero next-product
take, is a strong go-signal to pour into the attachment surfaces + the loop.

## 5. Reading the result through the confound (do NOT skip this)

The near-majority cohort **did not get the full Kiddo childhood** (18 years of
watching it grow, the accreting Memory Book). So this pilot strips out the very
attachment the moat is built on. That makes it a **lower-bound test**, and the
result is **asymmetric**:

- **Stays anyway → very strong.** If retention is good *without* the childhood
  moat, real born-into-Kiddo cohorts should do better. Believe a positive result.
- **Leaves → suggestive, not conclusive.** A cash-out could be the missing
  childhood rather than a broken thesis. Heed it as a yellow flag, dig into *why*
  (exit interviews), but don't fully condemn the moat on a no-childhood cohort.

Calibrate the kill line accordingly: it's set generously (1/3) *because* this
cohort is handicapped. A real cohort failing that bar later would be far more
damning.

## 6. The tracker (one screen)

Per kid: handoff date · kept? · active 6mo? · contributed? · Roth/next-product? ·
cashed-out date (if any) · one-line exit reason. Roll up to the five numbers.
Cross-reference against the `/admin` leading indicators (was a churned kid's
Memory Book thin? few gifters? low teen engagement?) — that's how you learn which
leading indicator actually predicts retention.

## 7. What NOT to do (the discipline)

- **No retention coercion.** No lock-ups, exit fees, guilt, or dark patterns. The
  money is legally theirs; a moat that needs handcuffs isn't a moat. Retention
  must be *earned* (`UNIT_ECONOMICS.md`).
- **No hand-holding the result.** Don't text a kid "please don't withdraw." You're
  measuring the product's pull, not your personal relationship's.
- **No moving the kill line after you see the data.** That's the whole point of
  pre-committing it.

## 8. Why this is worth more than another feature

You can keep polishing attachment surfaces forever, but until a real kid hits
majority and chooses to stay, the central question is unanswered. This pilot is
the only thing that answers it before year 18 — for the cost of your time and
~12 months. It is the highest-leverage de-risk available, and nothing in the
codebase substitutes for it.
