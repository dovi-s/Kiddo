## Before you send (60 seconds)
1. **Confirm the broker-dealer status** — the one fact the packet can't self-verify.
   The default framing is safe ("prospective candidate, no agreement executed").
   Only upgrade the wording if a BD is actually selected / an agreement signed.
2. **Attach:** the packet + a product overview/deck + current TOS draft + a
   pricing-page screenshot. Omit any custodian agreement (none is signed).
3. **Send to ~3 fintech/securities boutiques in parallel.** Pick the one that
   replies with a thoughtful scoping question, not just a fee quote.

## The email

**Subject:** Scoped fintech/securities opinion — custodial kids' investing platform (pre-launch)

Hi [Attorney / Firm],

I'm [Name], founder of Kiddo ([kiddofund.com]), a pre-launch consumer fintech. Kiddo
is a custodial investing product for children funded by family gifting: relatives
gift small amounts that get invested in a UTMA account a parent manages, and the
account transfers to the child at the age of majority.

I'm looking for a **scoped engagement to clear our pre-launch legal gate**. I've
prepared a single consolidated brief (attached) that pulls every open question into
one place, so this can be one engagement rather than several. I'm hoping for a
**60 to 90 minute call plus a 2 to 3 page memo**, and I'd like to move within about
two weeks.

**The structure I'd most like you to validate** (it is the question that most lowers
our cost and risk): Kiddo intends to be a **pure software and experience layer on a
rented, registered custodian / broker-dealer**. Gift money is intended to flow from
the gifter to the custodian directly and **never sit under Kiddo's custody or
control**, and the **0.10% annual asset-based fee is intended as a platform /
technology fee, not an advisory fee**. (Today, pre-custody, gifts route through our
Stripe only because there is no custodian to route to yet; the production target is
out of the flow. The custodian vendor is a prospective candidate and is not
finalized; no agreement is executed.)

**The decisive questions** (detail and structure in the attached packet):

1. **RIA registration.** If we operate as a **pure self-directed platform** (the
   gifter / parent / child affirmatively picks every security from a neutral menu,
   with no managed or age-banded allocations, no auto glide-path, no discretionary
   rebalancing), does the 0.10% asset-based fee trigger SEC RIA registration, or can
   it be framed as a platform / technology fee on a self-directed brokerage (the
   Stockpile / Public posture)? Where is the line between a neutral menu and a
   recommendation? (Part 1)

2. **Capturing money at the moment of intent.** Can we charge a gifter and hold or
   vault those funds **before the child's fund exists**, and if so for how long
   before we must auto-refund? This is the make-or-break for our model. (Part 2)

3. **Child data.** Our COPPA and child-PII collection / deletion posture for a
   product where minors are the beneficiaries. (Parts 3 and 5)

If the software-layer structure holds, does the same "platform, not a regulated
entity" finding also let us **launch nationally** rather than state-by-state? (Part 7)

The attached packet has the full framing, the specific sub-questions, and the
intended business structure. I'm reaching out to a few firms and will move quickly
with whoever comes back with a thoughtful scoping question.
