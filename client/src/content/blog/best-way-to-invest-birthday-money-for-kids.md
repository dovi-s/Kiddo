Birthday money usually arrives with good intentions and terrible follow-through.

A relative hands over a check, everyone smiles, and then the money sits on a counter until somebody remembers it again.

The better version is simple: decide before the party where the money is supposed to go.

## The better workflow

With Kiddo, you set up the fund once and share one private link. A birthday guest opens it, enters an amount, picks a stock or the ready-made mix, and sends, all in a few seconds. The family does not need to coordinate checks, cash, and Venmo notes after the party.

That is the whole point. Make the right thing easy while the occasion is actually happening.

## Why parents like this approach

- It is easier to share than brokerage instructions.
- It feels meaningful to grandparents and godparents.
- The gift can become an investment instead of idle cash.
- The Memory Book keeps the note and occasion attached to the money.

## Why gifters like it

- No account required
- Fast checkout
- Clear sense that the gift is doing something real
- A better story than slipping cash into a card

## The setup that works best

1. Create the fund a week or two before the party.
2. Add the link or QR code to the invitation.
3. Share the link again in the family group chat the morning of the party.

That is usually enough to catch both the planners and the last-minute gifters.

## Why this beats the envelope

The envelope tends to disappear into household clutter. The investment route turns the same gesture into something that can still matter years later, with the note and occasion kept alongside it.
