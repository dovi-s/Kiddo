This is one of the most common questions parents ask when they start thinking about investing for their child.

UTMA or 529?

The honest answer: for many thoughtful families, both.

## Where a 529 wins

A 529 is specifically built for education savings.

- potential state tax advantages
- tax-free qualified growth for education use
- a cleaner fit when college is the primary goal
- gentler treatment on the financial aid formula when parent-owned

If you are primarily saving for school and you are contributing systematically yourself, a 529 deserves serious consideration.

The old fear of overfunding a 529 has also softened. Under the SECURE 2.0 Act, unused 529 money can be rolled into a Roth IRA in the child's name, up to a lifetime cap of $35,000 and subject to conditions like a 15 year account holding period and the child having earned income. The "what if they do not go to college" worry now has a real answer.

## Where a UTMA wins

A UTMA is more flexible later.

- no education-only restriction
- simpler mental model for broader future support
- stronger fit when family gifting is part of the plan

That flexibility is the biggest reason many families still want one.

## The financial aid difference, plainly

If your family expects to qualify for need-based college aid, the two accounts are not treated the same. A UTMA counts as the student's asset on the FAFSA and is assessed at up to 20 percent. A parent-owned 529 is assessed at a maximum of about 5.64 percent.

That difference is the strongest argument for the 529 side, and it deserves its own honest treatment: we wrote one in [Does a UTMA hurt financial aid?](/blog/utma-financial-aid-fafsa) Families not expecting need-based aid can usually set this factor aside.

## The practical rule

If your only goal is education-specific savings, a 529 is worth serious consideration.

If your goal is making gifting simple and giving the child more flexibility later, a UTMA is usually the better fit.

Many families use both:

- a 529 for their own systematic college saving
- Kiddo for the family-gifting side
