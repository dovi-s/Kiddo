The hardest part is usually not opening the account.

It is asking people to change their gifting habit.

That is why the script matters.

## Keep the ask simple

Do not make it a financial lecture. Make it easy for people to say yes.

> We set up a Kiddo fund for Emma, so if you want to give something that lasts, you can use this link instead of bringing another toy.

That is enough.

## Why this works

- It respects the relationship.
- It tells them exactly what to do.
- It frames the gift as meaningful, not restrictive.
- It does not make the parent sound preachy or complicated.

## When to send it

- In birthday invitations
- In baby-shower notes
- In the family group chat before holidays
- In a follow-up message when relatives ask what the child wants

The best time is before people have already bought something.

## What not to do

- Do not over-explain UTMA rules in the ask.
- Do not send five different payment options.
- Do not make people create an account.
- Do not make the message sound like guilt.

You are giving family an easier, better option, not correcting them.
