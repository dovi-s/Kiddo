If you hold the shares in a brokerage account, you ask your broker to do it. Depending on the firm, they might make it easy or hard.

If you’re a US taxpayer, you’ll have to consider the gift tax implications. 200 shares of Nvidia is worth about $25,000. The annual gift exclusion amount is only $14,000. You can get around it if you own the stock jointly or gift it to your friend jointly with a spouse, or do it over two years. Or you could pay the gift tax.

Either way, your friend owes no tax on the gift.

Certainly, it's done all the time. Normally they would have to establish a brokerage or dividend reinvestment account in their own name and then the shares can be transferred to that account, though you could also ask to have a stock certificate sent to you instead. In that event, you would endorse the certificate over to your friend and send it to the transfer agent to be re-registered.

Bear in mind that I'm talking about shares held outside a retirement account. If you wanted to gift shares in an IRA to a friend, they are subject to distribution rules, taxation, and possible tax penalties.
