Path 1 (recommended): Everleaf is not the broker-dealer; you embed a broker stack

What this means

A regulated broker-dealer is the broker of record and performs KYC/AML, custody, statements, trade execution, tax forms.

Everleaf is the front-end product, event registry, payments UX, gift card, thank-you system, templates, referrals, and customer relationship.

Typical partners

Apex Clearing is an SEC-registered broker-dealer, FINRA member, and SIPC member, commonly used as clearing/custody infrastructure.

DriveWealth markets “Brokerage-as-a-Service,” and their customer agreement describes the “introducing firm” model where DriveWealth acts as a clearing broker-dealer for partner experiences.

Why this is the best for your product

You can truthfully say: “We open a brokerage account inside Everleaf, powered by [partner].”

You keep the “60-second gifting” magic because users do not get bounced out to Schwab/Fidelity flows.

Path 2 (advanced option later): Connect existing brokerage accounts

This is attractive in theory but usually kills conversion for gifting because it adds friction, matching issues, transfer delays, and support load.

It is best as “Already have an account? Connect it” after you have traction.

Broker-dealer of record: “Isn’t it whoever they connect to?”

Not in the embedded model.

In the embedded model, the user does not connect their Schwab/Fidelity account.

They open an account through Everleaf, and the broker-dealer of record is your embedded partner stack (or the introducing BD your program runs through), with clearing/custody handled by the clearing firm.

That is why the language you saw (“powered by Apex Clearing”) exists. Apex is explicitly positioned as a broker-dealer and SIPC member.

Compliance: what you actually need (in human terms)

You will have two layers: what the partner covers, and what you still must cover.

Your partner typically covers (because they are regulated)

Identity verification / CIP, KYC and AML program execution

Sanctions screening (OFAC-type checks)

Brokerage statements, trade confirms, tax reporting

Custody, clearing, settlement, corporate actions

Supervision of brokerage activity under their rules

Everleaf still needs (even if you are “just the app”)

Marketing + product compliance

Every claim on the site, every screen, every push notification, every referral incentive

Clear disclosures: “investing involves risk,” “values can go down,” “who holds the assets,” “fees”

No “performance promises” and no “beat bonds” positioning

Payments + fraud controls

Card fraud, chargebacks, stolen cards at events

Velocity limits, risk scoring, disputes process

This can be a bigger cost center than people expect early on

Data security + privacy program

Encryption, access control, audit logs, incident response plan

Vendor security reviews (your broker stack will require this)

SOC 2 is not day 1, but your controls should be SOC 2-ready

Customer support + complaint handling

Broker-dealer partners care a lot about complaint workflows and response times

Recordkeeping

You will be required (by contract and often by regulatory pass-through) to retain certain comms and transaction-related records.

Insurance and protection you should plan for

Even with a partner broker-dealer, you need to protect the company.

Cyber liability (table stakes)

Crime / fraud coverage (social engineering, payment fraud)

E&O / professional liability (especially if you drift into advice-like territory)

D&O (once you have real investors/board)

General liability (basic)

Your brokerage partner will have their own coverage and SIPC membership for brokerage accounts, but that does not replace your company’s coverage needs.
