some thoughts to consider: “Registry” is the UX people already understand, but the underlying gift is an investment position (shares or fractional shares), not a cash transfer.

Guests give dollars. Your platform turns those dollars into fractional shares (or holds them briefly as “uninvested cash” until the recipient picks what to buy). Fractional-share mechanics are already a standard pattern at major providers. Schwab Brokerage+2Stockpile+2

What exactly am I gifting?

You support 3 modes (all on the same event page):

1) Gift a specific stock or ETF (most literal “share gift”)

Guest picks: “$75 of Apple” or “$150 of VTI” (or “1 share of X” where possible).

Recipient ends up owning fractional/whole shares in their brokerage account.

This is how existing “gift stock” flows work: either fractional share purchase or a transfer of shares between accounts, depending on provider. Schwab Brokerage+2Fidelity+2

2) Gift into a “Grow Basket” (best default for most people)

Recipient sets a default basket for the event, examples:

“Core Growth” (broad index ETF)

“College Seed” (age-based or simple index)

“Home Down Payment” (more conservative mix)
Guests just choose an amount. The platform invests into that basket automatically, so it never feels like cash.

3) Hybrid: gift now, recipient decides later (solves the “I don’t want to pick stocks” problem)

Guest gives $100 as “Seed.”

Seed converts into shares once the recipient chooses their default (or after a set rule like “auto-invest into Core Growth after 14 days”).
This preserves the “not cash” feel while removing decision friction for the giver.

Stock gifting products already use “gift card converts into fractional shares when redeemed” as a model. Stockpile+2Stockpile+2

How the registry works (weddings, birthdays, graduations, bar mitzvahs, baby showers, anything)

Recipient setup (2 minutes)

Create event page (name, date, photo, short “why this”).

Choose default gift type:

Specific ticker list (optional)

Basket (recommended)

Seed (recipient decides later)

Optional goals: “$5,000 toward future” + milestones.

Share link or QR.

Giver flow (under 30 seconds)

Search event or open link.

Pick amount.

Pick “what it becomes” (stock, basket, or seed).

Pay (Apple Pay, card).

Add message.

They receive a clean receipt showing what was purchased (example: “$100 -> 0.43 shares of VTI at closing price”), which makes it feel real.

Recipient receives

A dashboard showing:

Total received

Contributions list (for thank-yous)

Holdings view (shares, performance, history)

Optionally: “Do not show performance to guests” (privacy)

The “not cash” rule (but keep an escape hatch)

Make “cash out” either:

Not available by default (strong stance), or

Available only into a linked bank after a cooling-off period, or

Available only for certain buckets (ex: “Honeymoon fund” is cash, “Everleaf portfolio” is shares)

This keeps the brand promise intact while avoiding edge-case frustration.

How this works mechanically (real-world rails)

There are a few viable ways, and you can support more than one:

Partner with a brokerage/custodian and open accounts (the cleanest “platform” version)

Recipient gets a brokerage (or custodial) account inside your product.

Gifts become fractional shares.

This mirrors what large brokerages already enable (fractional share purchase, gifting flows). Schwab Brokerage+1

Broker-to-broker share transfer (for sophisticated users)

Gift-giver transfers shares they already own.

Many brokerages support “transfer shares as a gift” workflows. Fidelity+1

Gift-code model that converts to shares (fastest to launch)

Similar to stock gift cards converting to fractional shares on redemption. Stockpile+2Stockpile Help Center+2

Important constraints you design around (so it doesn’t get weird)

Minors: you need custodial accounts (UGMA/UTMA style) or a dedicated minor flow.

Taxes: gifting shares has established rules (annual exclusion thresholds, cost basis transfer to recipient, etc.). Your product should present this as “FYI” not a tax lesson. Kiplinger+1

Volatility: you must set expectations clearly: “This can go up or down.”

Trust: receipts, provenance, “where the money went,” and extremely clear status (pending, invested, transferred).

The crisp version of the idea

A registry where every gift becomes ownership.

Guests can gift a specific share, a curated basket, or “seed” that converts into shares.

Recipient gets an event page, progress, thank-you tracking, and a real holdings account.
