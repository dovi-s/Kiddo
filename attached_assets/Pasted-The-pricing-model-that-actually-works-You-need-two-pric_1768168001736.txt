The pricing model that actually works

You need two price layers:

Payment processing (pass-through cost)
This is what Stripe (and the card networks) charge to move money.

Everleaf platform fee (your margin)
This covers brokerage partner costs, KYC, fraud, support, card design + delivery, receipts, thank-you automation, and your profit.

If you bundle these into one line without separating “cost vs margin,” you either lose money or look expensive and cannot explain why.

Pricing primitives (locked)
A) Payment methods
Card / Apple Pay

Processing (pass-through): Stripe card rate (example: 2.9% + $0.30)

Everleaf platform fee: 1.5% of gift amount (min $1, max $10)

So a $100 gift typically looks like:

Processing: $2.90 + $0.30 = $3.20

Everleaf fee: $1.50

Total fee shown to payer: $4.70

ACH (recommended for larger gifts)

Processing (pass-through): your ACH cost (example: $0.75)

Everleaf platform fee: 1.0% (min $0.75, max $10)

So a $250 gift:

Processing: $0.75

Everleaf fee: $2.50

Total fee: $3.25 (way cheaper than card)

UX rule: for gifts above $250, default to showing ACH as “Save on fees,” but keep Apple Pay one tap available.

Host plans (what the parent pays)

Important: the subscription is not “paying to invest.” It is paying to make the gifting experience better (fee coverage, premium cards, thank-you automation, templates, privacy).

$0/month

Create unlimited Funds and Events

Beautiful event pages + QR

Default “Future Fund” option

Contributors list + receipts

Basic thank-you export

Fees

By default: Guests pay processing + Everleaf platform fee

Optional toggle per event: Host covers Everleaf platform fee (guests still pay processing)

Host-cover pricing (when enabled):

Host is billed 1.5% (min $1, max $10) per gift that comes in on that event.

This is the cleanest “fair” model:

Guests always see real processing cost.

Hosts can choose to make checkout feel cheaper without Everleaf eating Stripe.

What else do you pay besides Stripe (your hidden cost stack)

Your Everleaf platform fee must cover these categories, or you will lose money even if Stripe is passed through:

Brokerage partner + clearing stack

Typical cost buckets (varies by partner):

KYC / identity verification checks

Account opening overhead

Money movement fees (ACH in/out)

Trade execution and settlement overhead (even if “commission-free”)

Monthly per-account platform fees at scale (sometimes)

You should assume this is meaningful. Do not price as if it is free.

Risk and support

Chargebacks and fraud tooling

Manual review and support headcount

SMS costs (thank-yous, receipts, verification)

Compliance legal and vendor monitoring

That is exactly why the platform fee exists.

“Do we make money if we bring accounts to the broker?”

Sometimes yes, but treat it as upside, not your core unit economics.

Possible upside streams (partner-dependent):

Revenue share on cash sweep yield

Securities lending revenue share

Referral bounties for funded accounts

Interchange later (if you ever add a card)

Do not rely on these to survive. They are gravy.

Your pricing must work even if partner economics are mediocre.

The simplest way to present fees (high conversion, low confusion)

On checkout show ONE line, with a tiny “details” link:

Fees: $4.70

Tap “details” reveals:

Processing (pass-through): $3.20

Everleaf platform fee: $1.50

Hosts understand. Guests do not feel tricked. Support tickets drop.

Does this cover paid-plan economics?

Yes, because Plus and Family are not subsidizing Stripe. They are primarily subsidizing your platform fee, which is your margin lever.

You can also add one more lever if needed later:

Optional “Truly fee-free checkout”

Hosts can choose to cover processing too:

Host pays processing + platform fee

Guests see $0 fees
Price it as:

“Host-paid fees” billed at actual processing + platform rate
This is an advanced toggle, not default.
