Thought for 8s
The core idea is strong: most bar mitzvah gifts are either disposable or cash that gets spent, and bonds often do not feel meaningful relative to how long the kid holds them. “Gift an asset” feels closer to what people intend when they give.

Where it already exists (and why it still feels unsolved)

You can already “gift stock” in a few ways (custodial brokerage accounts, UGMA/UTMA, some broker features, 529 contributions).

But the experience is usually not registry-like: it is clunky, not built for groups, not built for events, and not built for “aunt/uncle checkout in 30 seconds.”

If you build it as an event registry + checkout layer, you can make it feel like a modern gift product instead of a finance task.

The hard parts (the ones that decide if this is real)

Minor ownership and custody
A 13-year-old generally cannot directly own brokerage assets without a custodian structure (UGMA/UTMA, custodial trust, or parent-controlled account). Your UX must route through the parent/guardian.

Regulatory perimeter
The moment you facilitate buying securities, moving money, or holding assets, you are in broker-dealer and custody territory. The simplest path is usually to partner with a regulated brokerage/custodian and be an “introducing” or “front-end” layer. You still need KYC/AML, identity, fraud controls, disclosures, and a compliance program.

Taxes and reporting
Gifted securities have cost-basis rules, and dividends/1099s land somewhere. You need a clean story: who receives tax forms, how basis is tracked, what happens on sale, etc.

“I do not want to pick a stock” problem
Many gift-givers will freeze if you ask them to choose AAPL vs TSLA. You want simple defaults.

A product shape that can work
Think “registry for long-term gifts,” with three rails:

Rail A: Simple default baskets (high conversion)

“S&P 500 gift” (or total market)

Age-based glide path option (more conservative as they near college), similar to a 529 feel, but in brokerage form

Rail B: Pick a specific stock (for the people who want it)

Let the kid choose 5 companies they like, and gift-givers can pick one

Or “gift a slice of any public company”

Rail C: 529 contributions (the cleanest legal/tax option)

A 529 “registry link” is conceptually perfect for bar mitzvahs

It is also an easier compliance story than securities in many cases

You can offer both: “Investing gift” or “Education gift”

Group gifting: 10 relatives can jointly fund one basket
