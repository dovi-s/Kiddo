Agree. That “I just bought them a share of Tesla in 20 seconds” feeling is one of the strongest emotional hooks in the whole product.

The way to do it without breaking simplicity is:

The default stays simple

Primary CTA: “Gift $100 to their Future Fund” (auto-invests into the fund’s default basket).

That is what converts at scale.

Add “Pick a stock” as an optional lane (not the main lane)

On the giver page:

Amount presets ($36 / $54 / $100 / $180 / Custom)

Two choices:

Future Fund (recommended)

Pick a stock (optional)

Then show a search box with a short curated set first (TSLA, AAPL, VTI, etc.) plus search for any supported ticker.

This gives the dopamine without forcing everyone to make investing decisions.

How “buy the share” actually works (clean, real, compliant)

Because the giver is paying cash/Apple Pay, you are not transferring a share they already own. You’re doing a new purchase into the recipient’s brokerage account.

Mechanically:

Giver pays Apple Pay/card.

Money lands as “pending” in the recipient fund.

You place a fractional order for the chosen ticker into the fund’s brokerage account.

Once filled, the gift becomes “Invested” and the receipt updates with actual shares and price.

Key UX detail: you cannot promise “1 share” unless the gift amount covers it and the market price stays there. So you show:

“Estimated: $100 buys about 0.23 TSLA”

Then after execution: “Final: 0.2287 TSLA at $X.XX”

Also:

If market is closed, execute at next market open (or next available window). Show that plainly.

If the broker does not support fractional for a ticker, you block it or route to “Future Fund”.

What about “cash balance for recipient choice”?

Yes, but frame it as:

“Let them choose later” (Seed gift)
Default behavior: if they do nothing, auto-invest into the Future Fund after X days.

This avoids “it’s just cash” while still giving flexibility.

What you must show to avoid confusion

“Gifts may go up or down.”

“Orders execute after payment clears, during market hours.”

“Final shares may differ from estimate due to market price.”

For minors: “Parent/guardian controls the account.”

Naming

Call them Guests (event context) or Contributors (always-on fund context). Avoid “donor.”

If you do this, you get the premium “I gave them TSLA” moment, but the product still scales because the default is one-tap and the stock lane is optional.
