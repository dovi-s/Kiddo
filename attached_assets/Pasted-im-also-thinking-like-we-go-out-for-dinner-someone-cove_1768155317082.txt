Thought for 6s

This is a good idea as a “viral surface,” but it is dangerous if you implement it as “pick a ticker and send it” inside a payback flow.

Why it’s attractive

It turns Everleaf from “something you use at events” into “something you use any week.”

It creates a natural loop: someone receives a gift, installs the app, then uses it to send money back, then uses it for the next birthday.

It feels modern: “Do you want $50 back in cash, or should I drop it into your Future Fund?”

Why the naive version gets messy fast

If you let people repay friends in specific stocks (example “Disney”), you introduce:

Timing + price confusion (what price, what time, fractional rounding, market closed)

Support load (“I sent $50 of Disney but it shows $49.12”)

Tax and reporting complexity (now you’re effectively doing frequent small purchases/transfers)

Regulatory scope creep (it starts looking like payments + investing + money movement combined, which increases partner compliance friction and your operational burden)

The clean version that works

Make it an option, but keep it dumb and defaulted.

“Payback” only supports 2 destinations

Cash (standard repayment)

Their Everleaf Fund (not a ticker)

Default destination: their “Future Fund” (or whatever their default basket is)

Optional: “Education Fund” if that is one of their enabled rails

No stock picking in payback. If they want Disney, they can choose it later inside their own account.

UX that feels natural, not overloaded

In the payback sheet:

Amount: $50

Destination toggle:

Cash

Add to your Fund (recommended)

One line helper text:

“Fund deposits are invested into your default basket.”

Confirm

That’s it.

How it fits the product without changing the core

Your core product is still: event pages + gifting + funds for kids and families.

This becomes an “extra” you see only when:

you already have a contact in Everleaf, or

you are sending money to someone who already has a fund.

It’s optional, but it increases frequency.

Implementation guidance (important)

Treat this as Phase 2, not MVP.

Build it on the same rails as gifting:

money moves into the recipient’s account

it invests according to their default rules

receipts are clear (“$50 contributed, will invest next window”)

Avoid real-time trading promises. Use a clear status like: Pending → Invested.

Verdict

Yes, include it, but only in the “smart default” form: payback in cash or payback into their fund, not “payback in Disney.”
