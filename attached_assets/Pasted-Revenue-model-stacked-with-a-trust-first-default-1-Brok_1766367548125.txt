Revenue model (stacked, with a “trust-first” default)
1) Brokerage/clearing economics (primary engine)

If you run gifts through a broker-dealer/custodian partner, you can monetize via the standard brokerage revenue streams, while keeping pricing simple for users.

Securities lending / fully paid lending programs (where permitted and disclosed)

Cash sweep yield on uninvested cash (short windows still matter at scale)

Interchange on debit card (optional feature later)

Rebates / revenue share from your brokerage stack (varies by partner and product configuration)

This is how consumer brokerages typically make money without charging explicit trading fees. (Exact availability depends on your partner and regulatory setup, but the pattern is proven.)

2) Event “gift processing” fee (simple, transparent)

Two clean ways:

Option A: Giver pays fee

Example: “$100 gift + $2.50 processing”

Works because it maps to how wedding registries and ticketing already feel.

Option B: Host absorbs fee

Parents/couple cover fees so guests see a clean gift amount.

You can also offer “fee-free events” as a paid upgrade (see subscriptions).

3) Subscription tiers for hosts (parents/couples)

Subscription is not required for gifting; it upgrades the experience.

Free host

Event page + basic basket + thank-you export

Plus ($8–$15/mo or $49/event)

Fee-free gifts (or reduced fees)

Premium baskets (curated goals, smarter defaults)

Advanced privacy controls

Auto thank-you drafts + address collection

Multiple events per year

Family ($99/yr)

Multiple kids/events

Household dashboard

Recurring contributions (allow grandparents to “subscribe” $25/mo)

4) Premium “Baskets” and partner distribution

You can create themed portfolios (index-based, age-based, conservative) and earn:

Advisory/program fees (only if you offer advice via an RIA structure), or

Model marketplace revenue share (third-party model providers pay to be listed)

If you do anything that looks like personalized advice, you will likely need an RIA path; if baskets are generic, rules-based, and disclosed, it is simpler.

5) Gift add-ons (high-margin)

Digital + physical keepsake pack (printed “Seednote,” framed certificate, etc.)

Video message bundle

Premium card designs

Memory capsule (messages/photos associated with each gift)

This keeps the emotional “gift” part strong without making it feel like you are upselling finance.

6) B2B channel revenue (big scaling lever)

Sell to:

Event venues and planners (affiliate bounties)

Synagogues/churches/schools (fundraising + celebrations)

Employer perks (company-funded baby gifts, milestones)

College savings consultants

Offer rev-share: “X% of processing” or “$Y per activated household.”

Recommended pricing posture (so people don’t feel nickeled)

Always free to receive gifts

Small transparent fee on the giver (default)

Subscription removes/reduces fees + adds premium event tools

Back-end brokerage economics power the unit economics

Example unit economics (rough, but realistic structure)

Assume an average event:

35 gifts

$120 average gift

$4,200 total inflow

If you charge:

2.2% + $0.30 processing (blended, like typical card economics), and you keep ~1.0% net after card costs + partner fees
=> ~$42 net per event

Then add:

15% of hosts upgrade to $49/event “Plus”
=> +$7.35 blended per event

Then back-end brokerage economics on retained assets:

If 60% stays invested long-term and you earn a small annualized take-rate via partner rev share
=> becomes meaningful at scale.

What not to do

Don’t take a hidden spread without being explicit.

Don’t make the core feel like “cash registry with a stock logo.”

Don’t force subscriptions to do basic gifting. Package 1: Consumer-simple (fastest to market)

Goal: frictionless gifting, minimal choices, clear fee, no subscriptions required.

Pricing

For guests (default)

Card/Apple Pay gift: 2.9% + $0.30 per gift (standard “processing” pattern)

ACH gift: $0.75 flat (optional rail; can be “free” later)

For hosts (parents/couple)

$0 to create an event and receive gifts

What they get

Event page + QR

1 default “Grow Basket”

Thank-you list export (names, amounts, messages)

Seed mode optional (gift now, invest later)

Why this works

Matches mental model of donating/registries.

You do not need to sell anything to the host to grow.

When to ship

Day 1. This is your v1 pricing.

When to change it

After you consistently see:

High completion rates (guest checkout conversion strong)

Low refund disputes

Recipients actually invest (not just hold as cash)

At that point you can add Package 2.

Package 2: Premium-heavy (monetize hosts, remove guest friction)

Goal: make people love it, reduce guest fee friction, and turn hosts into recurring customers (multiple life events).

Pricing

Host plans

Plus

$49 per event (wedding, bar mitzvah, graduation, baby shower)

Includes: “Fee-free gifts” on that event up to $7,500 total gifts

After $7,500: host pays 0.75% (keeps you covered)

Family

$99 per year

Up to 6 events/year

“Fee-free gifts” up to $15,000 per year, then 0.75% host-paid

Guests

If event is “Fee-free,” guest sees $0 processing

Otherwise guest pays standard 2.9% + $0.30

What Plus and Family include (things people actually value)

Premium baskets (more options + better defaults)

Auto thank-you drafts + address collection

Gift privacy controls (hide amounts publicly, show only to host)

“Auto-invest rule” (ex: invest Seeds into default basket after 14 days)

Keepsake templates (printable card, “Seednote” PDF)

When to introduce it

Once you have:

A meaningful number of repeat events per household, or

Strong word-of-mouth with hosts asking “can I cover fees for guests?”

This is typically after product-market fit signals, not before.

Package 3: B2B-led (distribution + volume economics)

Goal: sell “event gifting as a service” to organizations that want a modern default for celebrations, and let them subsidize fees.

Target customers

Venues and event planners

Schools, alumni associations

Religious organizations (all types)

Employers (milestones: baby, marriage, anniversaries)

Fundraising groups (separate flow, but same rails)

Pricing options (pick one per vertical)

Option A: Platform subscription

$499 per year per organization

Includes 25 events/year

Additional events: $10/event

Gifts: guests pay standard fees unless the org subsidizes

Option B: Subsidized “fee-free” bundles

Org prepays $1,000

You cover guest fees up to $35,000 in gift volume (example cap)

Over cap: org pays 0.75%

Option C: Per-event licensing

$29 per event billed to the org

Guest fees remain standard

Referral economics (if you want a channel program)

Event planner gets $10 per activated event plus 10% of host upgrades

Venue gets $250 per 100 activated events (volume kicker)

Employer gets custom pricing, but target $5 to $15 per employee event depending on activation

When to introduce it

After v1 proves:

The gifting flow works reliably

Support load is manageable

Your compliance and operational rails are stable

Then B2B becomes a growth engine.

Recommended rollout sequence

Launch with Package 1 (consumer-simple, guest-paid fee)

Add Plus $49/event once hosts request “make it fee-free for guests”

Add Family $99/year once you see repeat events per household

Add B2B once reliability and support are under control

Concrete “default pricing” to put on the site (simple and clean)

“Create an event: Free”

“Guests can gift in seconds”

“Processing: 2.9% + $0.30 per gift”

“Want fee-free gifts? $49 per event”
