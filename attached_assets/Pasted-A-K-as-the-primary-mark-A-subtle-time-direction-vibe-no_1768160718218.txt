That is a really good brand cue because it reads as “Kora” first, and “future-forward” second.

What feels “slightly off” is almost certainly one (or more) of these very fixable issues.

What’s off in the current lockup
1) The K and the wordmark look like they come from different systems

The K looks like a UI icon. The “kora” looks like regular text. That mismatch creates the “something’s off” feeling.

Fix: make the wordmark feel like it was designed with the same intent as the K.

2) Spacing between mark and wordmark

The gap is either a hair too tight (feels cramped) or too wide (feels detached). Right now it reads slightly detached, like two separate things.

Fix: set a repeatable spacing rule (example below).

3) Vertical alignment

The K and “kora” may not be optically centered on the same baseline or cap-height. Even tiny misalignment looks amateur.

Fix: align optically, not mathematically.

4) Weight mismatch

Your K stroke weight and the wordmark weight do not match. If the K is thinner than the text, it feels weak. If it is thicker, it feels like an icon next to text.

Fix: match perceived weight.

The cleanest solution
Make the K an intentional monogram and make “kora” a custom wordmark-lite

Not a full custom font, just 2–3 tweaks:

Slightly tighten kerning: k o, o r

Maybe soften one corner or adjust the “k” terminal to echo the K mark

Keep it lowercase like this. Lowercase feels modern and friendly.

Recommended lockups

You should have all 3:

A) Primary horizontal (what you showed)

[ K mark ] kora

Rules:

Space between mark and wordmark: 0.75x to 1.0x the K’s stroke width (optical test)

Wordmark height: set so the x-height feels “equal energy” to the K, not literally equal height

B) Stacked (for app splash, print, social)

K on top, “kora” under it, centered.

C) Standalone mark (favicon, app icon)

Just the K, but you need a “small-size” cut where the inner negative spaces do not collapse.

If you want the “now vs later” to be intentional (without being cheesy)

Do it as an “easter egg” in the K, not as extra symbols beside it.

Two best executions:

Negative-space “>” inside the K where the diagonals meet.

Chevron-built K where the diagonals are subtly chevron-like, but still read as a letter.

Avoid literally putting < or > next to the K in the logo. That starts to look like code.

What I would change immediately

Make the K slightly more distinctive

Either a hidden “>” cut

Or slightly custom diagonals (not a default font K)

Use a slightly more “designed” wordmark

Same font family, but pick a weight that matches the K’s perceived weight

Tighten kerning carefully

Re-space and re-align
