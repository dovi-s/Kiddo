Yes - trading on someone else’s behalf without their consent is explicitly illegal.

There are tons of rules regulating brokers, dealers, investment advisors, and their agents/representatives and tons of exceptions to these rules, but almost all of them require disclosure and consent.

If you are a fiduciary and you are asking yourself whether you need to disclose something or receive consent, just remember that fiduciaries can never disclose too much or receive too much consent.

It is illegal as it is financial assistance provided for the acquisition of own shares.

It is only legal if such financial assistance (loan in this question) is made in the ordinary course of business (Ex. Company A is a bank or in money lending business).

You can borrow money from a Hongkong Bank and buy the Hongkong Bank shares in the stock market as money lending to you is in the ordinary course of business of Hongkong Bank. However, if Hongkong Bank grants you a very huge loan to buy an unusual significant amount of its shares, it is not in her ordinary course of business.
