You’re mixing three different “account layers” in your head. Once you separate them, everything clicks and becomes simple for users.

The 3-layer model
Layer 1: Everleaf (your product)

Everleaf is the UI + gifting/registry layer.

It creates Pages (links/QRs), runs checkout, stores messages, runs thank-you flows, and shows “where it’s invested.”

Layer 2: The brokerage account (where assets actually live)

This is the real securities account at your partner stack (example: Apex, Alpaca, DriveWealth).

This is where the cash + shares actually sit.

This is the thing that gets SIPC coverage (through the broker-dealer/custodian).

Layer 3: The clearing/custody + broker-dealer of record

This is the regulated entity that handles:

KYC/AML identity checks

custody of assets

trade execution/settlement

statements and tax docs

Everleaf should not ask users to understand Layers 2 and 3. Users only need to understand: “I have Funds. Each Fund has pages. Gifts go into the Fund and become investments.”

What is a “Fund” in Everleaf?

A Fund = one underlying brokerage account (almost always).

Examples:

“Mila’s Fund (Custodial UTMA)” -> underlying: Custodial brokerage account

“Dovi’s Fund (Personal)” -> underlying: Individual taxable brokerage account

“Wedding Fund” -> underlying: Joint taxable brokerage account (or one spouse’s, depending)

So your mental model is right:

People are not “sending stock to a page.”

They are contributing money that becomes investments inside the Fund’s brokerage account.

Pages are just “routes” (event templates) pointing at the same Fund.

Where do brokerages “play” in the product?

There are two product modes. Pick one for v1.

Mode A (recommended v1): Embedded account inside Everleaf

User creates a Fund in Everleaf.

Everleaf runs a short identity flow (KYC) inside your UI.

Behind the scenes, you open the brokerage account with your partner.

Gifts flow into that account and you place trades.

User experience: “I have a Mila Fund in Everleaf.” No talk of brokers.

Mode B (advanced later): Connect existing Schwab/Fidelity account

User already has Schwab/Fidelity.

They connect it and gifts route there.

This adds complexity and kills conversion. Good later, not day 1.

What does “send stock” mean in your UI?

For v1, don’t literally “send stock.” That creates confusion.

Instead, offer:

Send a gift (default)

Gift amount -> buys fractional shares of the Fund’s default basket or selected ticker

Optional later: “Transfer shares you already own” (in-kind transfer) is complicated and not needed to win.

So the “gift” is money that becomes shares, not a share-transfer between two brokers.

“Cash balance” vs “invested”

At the Fund level, there are always two states:

Cash: money received, not invested yet (pending, market closed, batching, or “Seed mode”)

Invested: holdings (ETFs, stocks) purchased with contributions

Your “Where it’s invested” should be Fund-level, because holdings live in the Fund’s brokerage account.
Event pages only affect attribution and messaging.

Multiple funds if I have kids

Yes. A household can have multiple Funds.

Household (you) can have:

Mila Fund (UTMA)

Noah Fund (UTMA)

My Fund (Individual taxable)

Each Fund has:

Always-on page (Open anytime)

Multiple event pages (Birthday, Bar Mitzvah, Graduation)
All pages point to the same Fund.

What about multiple account types like IRA + brokerage?

This is where you must be strict for simplicity and compliance.

Taxable brokerage (supported)

Great for gifts.

Anyone can gift money to it.

Easy.

Custodial UTMA/UGMA (supported)

Great for kids.

Gifts are irrevocable for the child.

Easy.

IRA (generally do NOT support for gifting)

IRA contributions have rules (earned income, annual limits, ownership constraints).

“Gifting into an IRA” is not a normal gift flow.

You can support “Retirement Fund” as a label but keep the underlying account taxable unless you want heavy constraints and user confusion.

So for v1: support only two Fund types

Custodial (kid)

Personal (adult taxable)

Everything else can be added later.

What “should happen” in common scenarios
Scenario A: Gift comes in on a weekend

Gift shows as Pending (market closed)

Cash sits in Fund cash balance

Next market open: auto-invest executes

Activity updates to “Invested”

Scenario B: Parent wants gifts held as cash until after event

Fund is set to Seed mode

Gifts accumulate in cash balance

Parent hits “Invest now” after event into default basket

Scenario C: Household has 2 kids and 5 events

One Fund per kid

Multiple pages per Fund

Dashboard shows Funds list

Each Fund expands to its events

Events show totals and share controls

Fund shows where invested

Scenario D: Adult wants both “personal investing” and “gifting”

Do not turn Everleaf into Cash App on day 1.

V1:

Adult has a Fund

Uses “Send a gift” to others

Add “peer-to-peer gift links” (still gifts, not payments)

Your dinner example (“cash or Disney?”) can exist later as a small feature, but it drags you into payments, disputes, and money transmitter complexity fast. Keep v1 pure: gifting + investing.
