Guessing you want to be within annual gift tax reporting guidelines?

In Fidelity - assuming your relative has an account# (and you know their full name, may be a bit more details)., simply call Fidelity rep and have them transfer X count of your MF/ETF shares (within Gift-tax reporting annual $$ limits?) to your relative account. Make sure NOT to check their box (on Fidelity end) - Mark this as Gift. By doing thus so., you preserve and transfer original cost based and date of purchase ~ and it be passed to the relative. If relative intends to sell those shares/etf ., they will realize LTCG. If they keep those - obviously, they will enjoy those dividends.

The key-point is NOT to check their box check-box (by the Rep., we don’t see this box): “is this a Gift”

If you somehow think needing to check this box — then, those shares be transferred at that day’s closing-price as your relative’s cost-basis. (guessing - you would owe/pay LTCG taxes on the basis difference- but, I have not explored much on this angle..)

Thanks to everybody for the help.

An update...
I've talked to Fidelity. They tell me a gift to another Fidelity account up to $32,000 per day can be done with a verbal authorization. Over that amount, or to another firm, requires paper with a Medallion guarantee. Since we have no Fidelity office closer than two hours away, that means getting the guarantee from a bank.

They said that verbal transfer to a Fidelity account is near instantaneous. Transfer to another firm requires mail time plus "several days" to process. So recipient is going to open a Fidelity account to facilitate the transfer.

My Fidelity account was set to "Average Cost Basis" for mutual funds. In order to transfer specific lots and control the recipient's capital gains tax (my holdings in this fund extend back nearly 60 years) I've had to change to "Actual Cost" basis and enter the basis for pre-2010 lots.

I gift appreciated stock to my kids every year to take advantage of their standard deduction - I immediately sell and buy VTI. Gifted shares carry the original basis, and my understanding is the gift amount is the average of the high/low on the day of the transfer. This would only matter if you're at risk of running up against the annual exclusion (including other gifts throughout the year).

What I do is call Fidelity and ask them to journal over X shares from specific lot(s) as gifts. As they are UTMA accounts with myself as the custodian they are easy to identify, but I expect that Fidelity would have no issue accepting the recipients name and account number for a transfer.

Update:

I just made my forth (of six) transfers by phone. The transfers show up as "pending" within minutes, and are completed overnight.

I've been told that there is a hard rule that telephone transfers cannot exceed $36,000 per day. That can be a bit of a bind for transferring mutual fund shares because the transfer is evaluated at the closing price that day. So if you set the number of shares too close to the $36,000 limit and the NAV of the fund goes up too much that day the entire transfer can be rejected.

I'm trying to give the gifted shares the highest basis possible so I'm selecting lots to achieve that. The agents have been very patient in selecting lots and splitting lots when needed to get my target transfer value for each day.
