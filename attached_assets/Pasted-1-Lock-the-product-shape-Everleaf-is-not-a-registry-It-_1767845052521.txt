1) Lock the product shape

Everleaf is not “a registry.” It is a gift layer on top of a real account, with beautiful “moment pages” that people share.

Entities (how the system is organized)

Household (or Personal)

One Everleaf login.

Can contain multiple Profiles.

Profile (who the money is for)

Child profile (custodial)

Adult profile (personal)

Fund (where gifts land)

Future Fund (default investing basket)

Seed (hold, then invest)

Later: Education Fund (529 rail), Separate because it is structurally different

Moment Page (the thing you share)

Bar mitzvah page, birthday page, graduation page, wedding page

Each Moment Page points to the same underlying Fund, but has its own:

design theme

copy

preset amounts

goal card (optional)

thank-you card style

One QR per Moment Page

That is correct. You can have one permanent QR for the Profile too, but Moments are what make it feel like a premium gift.

2) User types and what to call them

You need names that feel human and not fintech.

Recommended terms

Giver: “Contributor”

Parent: “Guardian”

Kid: “Recipient”

Adult user: “Owner” (or just “Member”)

Public audience: “Guests”

In UI copy, “Contributor” reads well: “Contributors can give in under a minute.”

4) Do contributors choose specific stocks?

You can support it, but it cannot be the default.

The right “dumbed down” offer menu

Default menu (v1):

Future Fund (basket, auto-invest, recommended)

Seed (hold then invest)

Optional (v2, only if you can keep it clean):

Favorites (guardian-approved list of recognizable brands)
This keeps emotional appeal without turning Everleaf into a trading surface.

Avoid a full “search any ticker” experience early. It adds support burden, compliance surface, and decision paralysis.

5) Should this also be “Cash App for stocks”?

Not at launch.

You have a much stronger wedge: life-event gifting + always-on kid funds.
If you nail that, you can later add:

“Send a gift” peer-to-peer as a gift, not as bill-splitting

Example: “Congrats” gift, “thank you” gift, “housewarming” gift

But do not position it as payments. Payments is a different battlefield and distracts from your strongest loop.

6) Brokerage integration and “do we make money from bringing accounts?”

You should assume two things at the same time:

A) You must be profitable without brokerage kickbacks

Partner economics are real, but you do not build a durable company by depending on them to cover your core costs.

B) You can often earn partner economics if you drive assets and accounts

Typical partner revenue sources can include:

account opening bounties or referral economics

revenue share tied to assets held or program participation (varies heavily by partner)

cash sweep economics on uninvested balances

securities lending participation (where applicable)

Treat this as upside, not survival.

The “best” integration choice

Use an embedded investing partner so account opening happens inside Everleaf and gifts become real investments automatically.
Do not send parents away to open accounts on a retail brokerage site if you want the seamless magic.

7) Revenue model that matches “fair, simple, trusted”

You need revenue in three layers.

Layer 1: Service fee on gifts (primary, always on)

One simple fee at checkout.

Card is higher, ACH is lower.

Host can choose guest-paid or host-paid.

This is what funds the business safely from day one.

Layer 2: Subscriptions (make people love you, increase conversion)

Plus (per event) buys premium tools + lower service fee

Family (annual) buys household + recurring + best fee rates

Subscriptions should not mean you subsidize Stripe. They should reduce your platform fee and unlock premium workflows.

Layer 3: Partner economics (upside)

Account and AUM-related economics, where available.
