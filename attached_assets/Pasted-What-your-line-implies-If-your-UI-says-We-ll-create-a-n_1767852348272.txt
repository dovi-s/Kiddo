What your line implies

If your UI says: “We’ll create a new brokerage account for you within Everleaf, powered by Apex Clearing. SIPC insured up to $500k,” that is the embedded brokerage model.

In that model:

Everleaf is not necessarily a broker-dealer.

A regulated entity provides brokerage services.

Apex Clearing Corporation is an SEC-registered broker-dealer and a FINRA + SIPC member (so it can be the brokerage services provider in some programs, and also the clearing/custody layer).

Lock-in: the “correct” $5B setup
Default (B): Embedded investing, Everleaf as the gifting layer

Users open an Everleaf-powered brokerage account in your flow.

Parent creates a Kid Fund (custodial structure for minors) or an adult fund.

Givers do not open accounts. They just pay and leave a message.

Gifts settle into the recipient’s Everleaf brokerage account and auto-invest per the chosen rail (Future Fund, Education, Favorites, Seed).

Why this is the winning default

Best conversion: no “go open a Schwab account and come back.”

You control the end-to-end experience: checkout, card reveal, receipts, thank-you, recurring, milestones.

Operationally consistent: fewer edge-case failures, fewer support tickets.

Later (C): Connect existing accounts, but as “transfer/consolidate”

The right version of C is:

“Already have a brokerage? Transfer your Everleaf holdings out later” (ACATS), or

“Consolidate an external account into Everleaf” (ACATS in).

The compliance truth in one paragraph

Everleaf should be the front-end product + payments + gifting UX. A partner broker-dealer/clearing stack handles KYC/AML, custody, statements, trade execution, regulatory reporting. The exact copy pattern you should use (so you do not accidentally claim you are a broker)

Use a disclosure block like:

Brokerage services: Provided by [Broker-dealer legal name], Member FINRA/SIPC.

Clearing and custody: Provided by Apex Clearing Corporation, Member FINRA/SIPC.

Everleaf: Everleaf is a technology platform and is not a broker-dealer.

Whether the broker-dealer of record is Apex itself or another entity depends on the specific program you sign, so keep it template-driven until you lock partners.

Cost implications you should assume (high level)

Even with embedded brokerage, you will have costs beyond Stripe, typically including:

Brokerage/clearing program fees (often per account, per trade, or basis-point style program fees)

ACH/wire costs and return risk

KYC/AML and identity verification costs

Customer support and disputes/chargebacks

Statements, tax docs, regulatory reporting overhead

That is why your pricing can’t just be “Stripe pass-through.” Stripe is only one line item.
