Your confusion is valid because you are mixing two different “numbers”:

Gift volume (how much money moves through the platform)

Everleaf revenue (what you keep)

In your scenario, gift volume is huge ($100k/month), but your revenue is small because you are charging a small take rate and also waiving fees on paid events up to a cap.

1) Re-stating your scenario in plain English

1,000 gifts/month

average gift $100

so $100,000/month is contributed into funds

5 events/month total

80% of that volume is on Free

Everleaf does not get to keep $100k. That money belongs to the recipients’ accounts.

2) What Everleaf actually earns (with the model we locked)
On Free

You earn 1.5% platform fee (capped at $10 per gift) on the gift amount.

Free gift volume: 80% of $100k = $80,000
Everleaf revenue from Free gifts: 1.5% × $80,000 = $1,200/month

Stripe’s 2.9% + $0.30 is paid by the giver as a processing fee. You do not keep it.

On Plus (one paid event)

Event volume: 20% of $100k = $20,000

Plus plan fee: $79 per event

Plus includes “waive platform fees” on the first $7,500 of that event.
So you only charge the 0.75% platform fee on the amount above $7,500.

Amount above cap: $20,000 − $7,500 = $12,500
0.75% of that: $93.75

Everleaf revenue from that Plus event:
$79 + $93.75 = $172.75/month

Total Everleaf revenue per month

$1,200 + $172.75 = $1,372.75/month

That’s the number I gave.

3) Is that “enough”?

For your scale goal, no. For this exact scenario, it is absolutely not enough.

Why:

1,000 gifts/month sounds big, but it is not big enough to support a heavy fintech stack.

Your revenue per gift in this scenario is:

$1,372.75 / 1,000 = $1.37 per gift on average.

If your all-in variable cost per gift is even $0.50 to $1.00 once you include support, fraud, ID checks (amortized), and brokerage program fees, you are not building a durable margin.

So either:

your scenario is too small for this model, or

the model needs more capture.

4) The right target economics for a $5B version

You want to be in a world where:

Average revenue per gift: $2.50 to $5.00 blended

or Average revenue per event: $75 to $200+ blended

plus meaningful recurring + Family plan adoption

Your scenario currently yields:

Revenue per event: $1,372.75 / 5 = $275 per event total across all events, but that’s misleading because it’s spread over 1,000 gifts.

Revenue per gift: $1.37

5) How to fix it without hurting conversion

You have 4 levers. Use 2, not all 4.

Lever A: Increase platform fee slightly on Free

Change Free platform fee from 1.5% cap $10 to:

2.0% cap $12

Impact on your scenario:
Free revenue becomes 2.0% × $80,000 = $1,600 (up from $1,200)
Net +$400/mo

This is a small change and still feels fair.

Lever B: Reprice Plus and Family upward (you underpriced them)

Plus at $79/event is likely too low if you are delivering “fee-free for guests” tooling, premium templates, and support expectations.

Suggested:

Plus: $99 per event

Family: $199 per year (or $149 if you want a softer entry)

That’s where premium registry products live.

Impact on your scenario:
Plus plan goes from $79 to $99 = +$20/mo (small in your mix)
Family doesn’t matter unless adoption increases.

The real reason to raise price: in real life, paid adoption will be higher than 20% of volume if the value is real.

Lever C: Tighten the “waiver cap” economics

Waiving platform fees on the first $7,500 is generous.

If you want safety:

Plus waiver cap becomes $5,000 (instead of $7,500)

Family waiver cap becomes $10,000/year (instead of $15,000)

This increases revenue on large events while preserving the marketing promise.

Lever D: Drive ACH usage for larger gifts

This improves your economics because:

fewer chargebacks,

lower processing burden,

better reliability.

You do this via UX:

“$250+? Save fees with bank transfer.”

6) What I would lock as “best” right now

If you want a safer, more profitable default without killing conversion:

Free

Platform fee: 2.0% cap $12

Guests pay processing

Plus

$99 per event

Platform fee waived up to $5,000

Over cap: 1.0% platform fee

Host covers processing when fee-free is enabled

Family

$199 per year

Waived up to $10,000/year

Over: 1.0%

This gives you materially better revenue per gift and more insurance against brokerage minimums.

7) The key point: your scenario is too small to judge $5B viability

A $5B version is not “1,000 gifts/month.” It is:

millions of gifts/month

heavy recurring gifting

multi-event households

and either a subscription base or a meaningful take-rate

In other words: the model works at scale, but you still need pricing that survives early-stage minimums and fixed costs.
