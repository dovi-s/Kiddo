How the Embedded Brokerage Model Works
Yes, it completely cuts out the traditional brokerage. Here's the deal:

Companies like Apex Clearing and DriveWealth are "infrastructure" brokerages. They're not consumer-facing — they provide the plumbing that apps build on top of. This is exactly how these apps work:

Robinhood → uses its own clearing (used to use Apex)
Stash → uses Apex Clearing
Acorns → uses Apex Clearing
Public.com → uses Apex Clearing
Stockpile → uses DriveWealth
Cash App Investing → uses DriveWealth
When you sign up for any of these apps, you're actually opening an account at Apex or DriveWealth — you just don't know it because the app wraps the whole experience.

The trades are commission-free to the end user. The clearing firm makes money on things like payment for order flow, securities lending, and cash sweep interest. Kora would make money on its own platform fees (the 1-1.5% Kora fee), not on trading commissions.

Should Kora Allow Connecting an Existing Brokerage?
This is a strategic decision. Here's the honest trade-off:

Option A: Embedded Only (Recommended for Kora)

User creates account through Kora → brokerage account opens invisibly via Apex/DriveWealth
Kora controls the full experience end-to-end
Gifting flow is seamless — cash in, stock purchased, all in one place
Simpler for the user (no "connect your Schwab" confusion)
This is what Acorns, Stash, Stockpile all do
Option B: Allow Connecting External Brokerages

Would use something like Plaid for brokerage linking
Massively more complex — every brokerage has different APIs, account types, transfer rules
The Reddit threads show how painful cross-brokerage transfers are ("My stock is at Schwab, the kid's accounts are at Fidelity... how do I do this?")
Would reintroduce the exact friction Kora is designed to eliminate
My recommendation: Embedded only. The whole point of Kora is that nobody has to deal with brokerage accounts. The moment you ask someone to "connect your Schwab," you've lost the magic. The account exists inside Kora, managed by a real clearing firm, fully regulated, SIPC-protected. The user never thinks about the brokerage — they think about their child's future.

Down the road, if a user wants to transfer out (move their holdings to Schwab or Fidelity), that's a standard ACATS transfer that the clearing firm handles. But that's an edge case for later, not a core flow.
