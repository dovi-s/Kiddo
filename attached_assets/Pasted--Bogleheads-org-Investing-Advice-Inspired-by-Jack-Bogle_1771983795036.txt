Gift Stock from a Roth
Post by AZKathy » Wed Jan 08, 2025 5:57 pm

I'm hoping you can help me find the correct answer to this.

Generous X (who is over retirement age and has held the Roth for more than 5 years) wants to give his adult nephew stock from his Roth. X thinks that his nephew can receive the stock in his brokerage account and then transfer it, as stock, into his Roth or IRA. (The nephew works and has enough income to cover it. All accounts are in Vanguard.) And because it is all a gift from a Roth X thinks/hopes there won't be a tax burden on the nephew.

So the questions are about stock gifts from a Roth--
Can you gift stock from your Roth to an individual?
Does the recipient have to sell the gift stock before putting it in his IRA or Roth? Are those rules different?
If so, does the recipient have to pay the capital gains when they sell it, since it is from a Roth? (The stock value has increased in X's Roth.)
Does the IRS count the gift as income when transferring a gift from a relative into a Roth? Or does the money somehow retain its properties as a gift.

I understand that X could just sell the stock in his Roth and gift the cash to the nephew but X wants to gift the stock. If, in the end X does sell the stock in his Roth and gifts the cash, does the nephew have to pay the tax before putting it in his Roth or can you put gifts in your Roth without incurring a tax, since it isn't income. (Assuming you have enough income to cover it.)

Thank you for any help you can give me. I have Googled this unsuccessfully.

Kathy

You can gift stock out of your account.

You can only contribute cash to a IRA or Roth.


The tax rules are identical if you gift cash or stock.

It would be simpler for everyone to liquidate the stock in your ROTH and then for the son to contribute cash to their ROTH.

Post by dukeblue219 » Wed Jan 08, 2025 6:01 pm

Since it is a Roth and gifter is eligible to make withdrawals, the simple option is to make a cash gift. Recipient will owe no tax.

There is no way to gift stock from an IRA, nor is there really a point in doing so. There is no way to receive a gift into a Roth to bypass contribution limits.

If one insists on gifting stock, the gifter must sell, get cash, buy stock in a taxable account, and gift from there into recipients taxable account.

No. Vanguard simply won't allow this type of transaction to occur.

The website won't let the source or the destination account be a Roth IRA or a Traditional IRA. It only works from taxable account to taxable account. If you're using taxable accounts on both sides of the transaction you can gift money market fund shares (i.e. cash) or you can give shares of a stock or mutual fund or ETF.
